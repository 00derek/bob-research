## How the business creates value

### Current assessment and evidence

The October 27 quarter and September cash statement remain the latest retrieved operating basis. The November 11 close remains outside the surviving prospective band and HIGH-event gap-adjusted reward/risk is below 2:1. Product or stablecoin headlines do not change common earnings assumptions or turn deposits into surplus equity cash. [Current primary basis](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1818874/000181887426000070/sofi-20260930.htm); [dated current check](https://investors.sofi.com/news/Press-Releases/).

Members pay interest and fees; banking relationships provide funding and engagement, while technology customers buy processing/software services. Deposits lower reliance on wholesale funding, but they are customer liabilities rather than equity cash. The loan-platform business originates or distributes loans for partners, potentially reducing capital needs while creating dependence on investor demand. More products per member can lower acquisition cost, yet only profitable incremental products improve value. Personal-loan demand, technology accounts and banking products have distinct unit economics and should not be treated as equal subscriptions.

The competitive constraint is observable in the model inputs: personal-loan coupon 12.78% must cover funding, servicing, defaults and capital; reported fair-value default assumptions rose to 5.10% from 4.77%. Banks, credit cards and marketplace lenders can compete on rate and distribution. [Happen’s October 28 Q3 release](https://ir.happen.com/news-events/news-releases/news-details/2026/Happen-Inc--Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) provides a current competitive check: originations rose 26% to $3.336 billion, deposits reached $11.5 billion and CET 1 was 15.7%. The former Lending Club completed its Happen rebrand in June. Its $263.2 million net revenue fell 1% partly because new originations moved to fair-value accounting from January 1; its 3.1% investment-loan charge-off measure cannot be directly ranked against SoFi’s sale-adjusted 3.4% personal-loan measure. The rival demonstrates active competition for borrowers, deposits and marketplace buyers, rather than proving a relative credit winner. Product breadth and cross-selling earn moat credit, with limited points because current peer pricing and retention data remain incomplete.

**Evidence gap:** Current product-level market share, contract margins and complete peer earnings revisions are not all disclosed or independently comparable. Competitive observations support the mechanism, but do not establish monopoly pricing or the exact terminal growth assumed.
