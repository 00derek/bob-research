## Investment thesis

SoFi combines digital financial services, lending and technology to earn more revenue from each customer relationship. Deposits can lower funding costs, while fee-based distribution and cross-selling can improve operating leverage. The October 27 result supports that mechanism, but credit losses, funding costs and dilution determine what reaches each equity unit. WATCH at $17.14 on October 28 reflects an improved FY2028 model that still offers less than the framework's 25% starter upside and trades above the previously declared $13–14 research zone. The strongest opposing case is that fee income and customer monetization expand faster than the base forecast while credit stays benign. The competing downside is that rapid loan growth makes current margins look better before losses season. Both need evidence; a sharp post-earnings rally settles neither.

## How the business creates value

Lending earns interest and origination/distribution economics; Financial Services monetizes customer relationships; technology services supply financial infrastructure. A member using more services can improve acquisition-cost efficiency, but product counts do not equal incremental profit. The bank funding model can be a competitive advantage when deposits are stable, while regulation and credit underwriting constrain the rate of growth.

October 27 Q3 results reported roughly $1.4 billion net revenue and $241 million GAAP net income, versus $139.4 million net income a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA was $470 million and adjusted EPS $0.18. Management introduced a FY2027 outlook of $6.4 billion adjusted revenue and $0.90 EPS. These are issuer forecasts, not an assurance of FY2028 cash earnings. Credit provision, fair-value marks, loan sales and capital requirements must be reviewed together; deposits are financing liabilities, so a simple industrial-company net-cash DCF would be inappropriate. [Primary evidence](https://investors.sofi.com/news/news-details/2026/SoFi-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-with-Record-Net-Revenue-of-1-4-Billion-Net-Income-of-241-Million-and-Raises-Full-Year-Guidance/default.aspx).

The refreshed base model assumes $8 billion FY2028 revenue, 20% normalized net margin and 1.45 billion diluted units, yielding about $1.10 EPS. It extends management's FY2027 sales outlook by 25% and allows dilution rather than simply dividing by today's basic count. Bear sales of $5.8 billion and a 10% margin stress credit and funding; bull sales of $10 billion and 23% margin require stronger fee mix and execution. These margins are analyst normalizations and should not be confused with adjusted EBITDA margin.

## What could break the thesis

Credit losses can lag originations, and fee income may depend on third-party demand for loans. A weaker economy could raise defaults and funding costs together. A material reconciliation failure in loan valuation or credit disclosures would reopen the credibility gate; this review identifies no verified new allegation that establishes failure. Observable warning signs are worsening cohort losses, weaker loan-sale execution and dilution outrunning earnings growth.

Thesis **INTACT**; credibility **PASS**; GAAP pre-profit **No**; binary-event risk **MEDIUM**. The 25% plausible overnight gap is an analyst stress, not a forecast bound. A daily-close stop cannot guarantee execution at its level. For HIGH binary risk, the larger of stop distance and gap stress governs sizing.

**Evidence gap:** Complete current gap distributions, material capital claims and synchronized insider/short evidence are unavailable. Test the specific business failures above and refresh immediately on results, financing, governance or thesis-changing news.
