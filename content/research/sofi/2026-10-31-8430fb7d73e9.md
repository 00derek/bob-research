## Investment thesis

SoFi converts member relationships into lending spreads, banking fees and technology revenue. The October 27 quarter shows stronger monetization, real common earnings and a credible near-term growth guide. The new October 31 model allows further growth without assuming every loan mark is permanent or every deposit is surplus cash. Its weighted estimate is above the October 30 price, but the new accumulation zone must first receive a future completed close because it is declared today. WATCH is the current security signal, with only a conditional future starter assessment. The favorable case is that existing members add products and the loan platform expands fee earnings without proportional balance-sheet capital. The opposing case is that credit losses, deposit competition and fair-value reversals erase the operating leverage. Bank capital and common dilution matter more than a headline EBITDA margin.

## How the business creates value

Members pay interest and fees; banking relationships provide funding and engagement, while technology customers buy processing/software services. Deposits lower reliance on wholesale funding, but they are customer liabilities rather than equity cash. The loan-platform business originates or distributes loans for partners, potentially reducing capital needs while creating dependence on investor demand. More products per member can lower acquisition cost, yet only profitable incremental products improve value. Personal-loan demand, technology accounts and banking products have distinct unit economics and should not be treated as equal subscriptions.

The competitive constraint is observable in the model inputs: personal-loan coupon 12.78% must cover funding, servicing, defaults and capital; reported fair-value default assumptions rose to 5.10% from 4.77%. Banks, credit cards and marketplace lenders can compete on rate and distribution. [Happen’s October 28 Q3 release](https://ir.happen.com/news-events/news-releases/news-details/2026/Happen-Inc--Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) provides a current competitive check: originations rose 26% to $3.336 billion, deposits reached $11.5 billion and CET1 was 15.7%. The former LendingClub completed its Happen rebrand in June. Its $263.2 million net revenue fell 1% partly because new originations moved to fair-value accounting from January 1; its 3.1% investment-loan charge-off measure cannot be directly ranked against SoFi’s sale-adjusted 3.4% personal-loan measure. The rival demonstrates active competition for borrowers, deposits and marketplace buyers, rather than proving a relative credit winner. Product breadth and cross-selling earn moat credit, with limited points because current peer pricing and retention data remain incomplete.

**Evidence gap:** Current product-level market share, contract margins and complete peer earnings revisions are not all disclosed or independently comparable. Competitive observations support the mechanism, but do not establish monopoly pricing or the exact terminal growth assumed.

## What could break the thesis

Cumulative loan fair-value premium fell from $2.027 billion in June to $1.399 billion in September despite growing principal; higher benchmark rates and default assumptions explain part of the change. A full haircut is not automatically a future realized credit loss, but at about 14% of tangible book the remaining mark is material. Cross-selling could be less profitable than member counts imply, and partner demand for loan distribution can disappear in stress. Worsening seasoned-vintage losses, funding outflows, capital falling through the planned buffer or persistent fee/customer churn would invalidate the base.
