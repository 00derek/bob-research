## Business economics and competitive position

Nebius designs and operates GPU infrastructure and a software layer for AI training, inference and related services. Customers pay for usable compute capacity, not for a signed power reservation. Hardware/software integration, deployment speed and contracted power can differentiate it. Hyperscalers and other specialist clouds compete for the same customers, equipment, power and financing. Customer prepayments can fund construction, but also create future service obligations and concentration risk.

Competitive growth is real but does not establish protected returns. [CoreWeave's August 10 primary financing release](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Closes-2-6-Billion-Loan-Facility-Expanding-Financing-Flexibility-for-AI-Infrastructure/default.aspx) reports another $2.6 billion facility for its AI infrastructure. [AWS's October 29 results](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2026/Amazon-com-Announces-Third-Quarter-Results/) show 39.3% cloud growth. Rivals can expand supply and have substantial commercial and financing resources. Nebius's capacity and engineering are advantages; it does not yet have demonstrated hyperscaler-like cash resilience.
