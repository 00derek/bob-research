## Business economics and macro transmission

Chipmakers buy systems, service and upgrades to increase wafer output, improve yields and reduce manufacturing cost. Switching the critical lithography process is difficult, especially at advanced nodes. ASML's installed base also generates recurring service and field-option sales. Customer construction schedules, power, process adoption and export licenses can nevertheless delay delivery or revenue recognition even when demand is real. Nikon and Canon remain alternatives in some mature lithography categories; the broader semiconductor-capital cycle competes for the same customer budgets.

The quarter ending **September 27** produced **€11.662 billion sales**, versus **€7.516 billion** a year earlier and **€9.326 billion** in June. Service and field-option sales were **€3.289 billion**, up from **€1.962 billion** a year earlier. Gross margin reached **57.5%**, with **€5.173 billion operating income**. Reported net income of **€4.960 billion** includes a **€509.7 million equity-investment gain**, identified on the call as the increased value of Mistral AI. It should not be treated as additional recurring lithography earnings. Quarterly diluted EPS was **€12.93**, distinct from the **€12.95 basic EPS** in the release headline. [Full financial statements, Exhibit 99.3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828026065813/financial_statementsxusx.htm).

Full-year sales guidance is **€44–45 billion**, with approximately **56% gross margin**. Management says customer demand could exceed planned 2027 and 2028 capacity; 30% low-NA EUV/DUV-immersion capacity increases are discussed for each year, with additional 2028 options under investigation. Capacity growth is not guaranteed sales growth, nor is a customer's expression of interest a completed order. Value-based pricing can help margins, but management explicitly did not promise to repeat exceptional pricing negotiations every year.
