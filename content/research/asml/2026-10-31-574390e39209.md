## Investment thesis

ASML supplies lithography equipment and upgrades that enable chipmakers to print advanced semiconductor patterns. Its EUV technology, supplier network and installed base create a difficult-to-replicate position. That business quality is separate from the price paid. The newly rebuilt October 31 assessment is **WATCH at $1,565.90**, the October 30 Nasdaq close. New scenarios give **$1,817.09 weighted present value**, approximately **16.0% upside**, below the 25% starter requirement. The strongest opposing case is that higher lithography intensity, capacity growth and value-based pricing sustain superior growth for years. The model includes that opportunity while removing the latest quarter's investment-mark gain and distinguishing customer downpayments from recurring cash profit. [October 14 issuer results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828026065813/pressreleasefinancialresul.htm).

## Business economics and capacity

Chipmakers buy systems, service and upgrades to increase wafer output, improve yields and reduce manufacturing cost. Switching the critical lithography process is difficult, especially at advanced nodes. ASML's installed base also generates recurring service and field-option sales. Customer construction schedules, power, process adoption and export licenses can nevertheless delay delivery or revenue recognition even when demand is real. Nikon and Canon remain alternatives in some mature lithography categories; the broader semiconductor-capital cycle competes for the same customer budgets.

The quarter ending **September 27** produced **€11.662 billion sales**, versus **€7.516 billion** a year earlier and **€9.326 billion** in June. Service and field-option sales were **€3.289 billion**, up from **€1.962 billion** a year earlier. Gross margin reached **57.5%**, with **€5.173 billion operating income**. Reported net income of **€4.960 billion** includes a **€509.7 million equity-investment gain**, identified on the call as the increased value of Mistral AI. It should not be treated as additional recurring lithography earnings. Quarterly diluted EPS was **€12.93**, distinct from the **€12.95 basic EPS** in the release headline. [Full financial statements, Exhibit 99.3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828026065813/financial_statementsxusx.htm).

Q4 guidance is **€14.2–15.2 billion sales**, approximately **57% gross margin**, **€1.2 billion R&D** and **€0.4 billion SG&A**. Full-year sales guidance is **€44–45 billion**, with approximately **56% gross margin**. Management says customer demand could exceed planned 2027 and 2028 capacity; 30% low-NA EUV/DUV-immersion capacity increases are discussed for each year, with additional 2028 options under investigation. Capacity growth is not guaranteed sales growth, nor is a customer's expression of interest a completed order. Value-based pricing can help margins, but management explicitly did not promise to repeat exceptional pricing negotiations every year. [Full October call](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/ams/ASML/transcripts/651248-q3-2026-investor-call/).

The ASML–TSMC initiative for larger High-NA photomasks provides customer-side evidence of continued process development; it is not an independently measured 2028 sales forecast. China was described at approximately 20% of system revenue for 2026, with licensing and policy exposure remaining material. **Evidence gap:** Exact customer demand conversion, tool acceptance timing and a synchronized industry revision panel were not verified. Orders involve downpayments and contractual commitments, but cancellations, deferrals and export restrictions still matter. [TSMC joint announcement](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news/3338).

### Cash and reinvestment feasibility

The €75 billion base sales path requires more than tool scarcity. An illustrative **55.5% gross margin**, **10% R&D/sales (€7.5 billion)** and **3% SG&A/sales (€2.25 billion)** produces **€31.875 billion operating profit**, or **42.5% operating margin**. At **17% ordinary tax**, plus approximately **€0.25 billion net financing income and €0.30 billion recurring equity-method profit**, common earnings are about **€27.01 billion**, consistent with the rounded **36% net margin**. The €509.7 million Mistral revaluation is excluded.

A 2028 cash test starts with €27 billion earnings, adds **€1.6 billion depreciation**, removes the **€0.3 billion equity-method earnings** until distributed, and deducts **€5 billion working-capital investment**, without any new net customer-downpayment benefit. CFO is approximately **€23.3 billion**. PPE capex of **€5 billion**, more than three times the current annualized pace, plus **€0.25 billion intangible investment** leaves about **€18.05 billion cash after those investments**. Dividends **€3.5 billion**, repurchases **€8 billion** and debt repayment **€2 billion** leave approximately **€4.55 billion**. A 2027 test at €58 billion sales/35% margin, €1.3 billion depreciation, €0.3 billion equity-method earnings, €4 billion working capital, €4 billion PPE and €0.2 billion intangibles leaves about **€13.1 billion**, before €3 billion dividends, €6 billion repurchases and €1 billion debt repayment. Thus the base funds substantial capacity investment and engineering without repeating the latest €7 billion quarterly working-capital inflow.

These are analyst cash budgets, not issuer capex guidance. A **€5 billion additional reversal of existing customer funding** would consume essentially all the 2028 residual; a €10 billion reversal would require roughly **€5.45 billion** liquidity before other changes. The September €12.735 billion cash/investment balance is a buffer, subject to its other fulfillment obligations. Higher investment or slower collections should reduce repurchases before being labeled distributable profit. **Evidence gap:** Supplier payments, actual factory expansion costs and downpayment reversals are not fully scheduled publicly; the model requires execution and retains that uncertainty.
