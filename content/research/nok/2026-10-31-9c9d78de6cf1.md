## Investment thesis

Nokia sells the optical, IP and mobile infrastructure that carries network traffic. AI data centers create a growing need for high-capacity connections, offering a route to faster sales beyond the slower telecom equipment cycle. October 22 results support that demand, but the gap between comparable and reported profit, plus weaker cash conversion, limits the value of an earnings-only narrative. At the $8.56 October 30 ADR close, the rebuilt conditional weighted estimate is $7.25, about 15% below price. The security assessment is **Watch**: no entry qualifies at this quote. The central dependencies are profitable optical/IP growth and the normalization of restructuring, integration and working-capital needs. The strongest opposing case is that strong orders produce sustained cash and higher normalized margins. That would challenge the cautious valuation, but excluded costs cannot simply be assumed to vanish.

## How the business creates value

Optical and IP networks move data between cloud facilities and across carrier networks; mobile infrastructure links users and devices. Customer relationships, technology qualification and installed networks support demand, while large telecom/cloud buyers can influence pricing. Infinera integration may improve the optical offering but introduces costs and execution complexity. Mobile capex remains a different cycle from AI networking.

The [October 22 Q3 issuer report](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/nokia-corporation-interim-report-for-q3-2026/) reports €5.154 billion sales,€605 million comparable operating profit and 11.7% comparable margin, versus 3.8% reported margin. AI/cloud customer sales grew123%. Net cash and interest-bearing investments were €2.584 billion. Management retained €2.1–2.6 billion full-year comparable operating profit, but cut free-cash-flow conversion to 35–55%, including additional working capital and accelerated restructuring.

The new FY2028 base revenue €24 billion and 9% normalized common net margin imply €2.16 billion common earnings before dividing by 5.65 billion diluted units. That requires charges to normalize and cash conversion to improve; it is not management's guide. Reported common earnings remain well below that path. **Evidence gap:** The detailed acquisition/restructuring, cash conversion and fully diluted ADR bridge is incomplete. A normalized earnings model can express the conditional case but cannot establish that the adjustments are nonrecurring.

## Macro transmission

Rates affect carrier financing, cloud capex and the discount on future network earnings. Inflation raises manufacturing and deployment costs. Liquidity helps customers invest but can be consumed in working capital before payment. AI networking demand is secular, while telecom budgets remain cyclical. Export restrictions, national-security procurement and supplier geography change market access.

The October 31 shared macro assessment is **8/25**: rates 1/5, inflation 1/5, liquidity 2/5, cycle 3/5 and policy 1/5. The October 30 S&P 500 closed at 7,580.07 and Nasdaq at 25,437; the selloff weakens liquidity and risk appetite. September PCE released October 29 showed 3.6% headline and 3.1% core annual inflation, while third-quarter real GDP grew at a 3.4% annual rate. Persistent inflation and growth leave long-duration earnings exposed to discount-rate pressure. The 50% normal-size cap and starter/add-only limit remain; macro modifies size rather than company conviction. [BEA PCE](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-september-2026); [BEA GDP](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-3rd-quarter-2026).

**Evidence gap:** The five economic channels above are qualitative sensitivities; no verified company-specific elasticity to rates, energy or liquidity is claimed. Quantify those effects in the operating scenarios if they materially change margins, financing or demand.
