## How the business creates value

Electron/HASTE sell launch access; spacecraft and components sell mission capability. Neutron opens larger payloads but adds development and reusable-launch execution risk. Iridium brings spectrum, distribution and service subscriptions whose replacement-capital requirements differ from manufacturing. Vertical integration can reduce external launch expense while creating dependence on internal schedules. SpaceX is a capable integrated competitor; government programs can favor second-source capability without guaranteeing a particular share or margin. Internal launches must not be double-counted as external revenue. The combined forecast separates launch, systems and communications before calculating equity profit.

Iridium's Q3 filing provides a useful test of the communications business being purchased: nine-month operating cash flow was $281.706 million versus $291.478 million a year earlier, while capex rose to $79.238 million from $66.791 million. Simple cash after capex fell to $202.468 million from $224.687 million, before the Aireon acquisition payment, dividends and financing. This is actual cash conversion; OEBITDA is not substituted for it. [Iridium September 30 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1418819/000141881926000052/irdm-20260930.htm). **Evidence gap:** Current competitor launch price, contract-level margins and satellite replacement cost comparisons are incomplete. SpaceX competitive strength is clear from overlapping markets, but no unverified market-share forecast is credited.

The issuer confirms November 9 results are still upcoming. September 15 financing disclosure targets Iridium closing in mid-2027 subject to regulatory approvals. Acquiring its cash generation is therefore a conditional combined scenario, not current consolidated revenue. The October Neutron delay into 2027 remains the central cadence risk. No new cost-to-complete schedule was recovered, so no improvement is assumed.
