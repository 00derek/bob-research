## Investment thesis

Vishay sells the power and passive components needed across industrial, auto and AI systems. Its latest released quarter shows an order and margin recovery, but October 31 falls before the November 4 Q3 announcement. This rebuilt model uses actual Q2 statements, the subsequent revolver repayment and the cash-settled principal obligation on convertible notes. WATCH follows a discounted estimate near the current price, with insufficient entry upside and weak technicals. The favorable case is that additional capacity, pricing and broader customer access sustain higher utilization. The opposing case is that longer backlog includes precautionary orders while capex, metal costs and cash conversion disappoint. The July equity raise funded flexibility by diluting common claims; it was not operating cash.

## How the business creates value

Discrete semiconductors switch or control power while resistors, inductors and capacitors enable circuits to function. Qualification and reliability matter, but product substitution and competing suppliers limit pricing. Backlog of US$1.877 billion/6.1 months and book-to-bill 1.32 show orders ahead of shipments; they do not guarantee firm end-customer demand. Distributor inventories fell to 18 weeks from 20, but customers seeking assured supply may order early. Newport qualification, fab loading and packaging capacity determine how much incremental sales earn. [Q2 call and channel data](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/vsh/transcripts/668082-q2-2026/).

STMicroelectronics October 29 release shows US$3.76 billion revenue, 17.9% yearly growth and 37.3% gross margin. Its broad recovery supports demand but its different semiconductor mix cannot prove Vishay passive-component share gains. TI industrial/data-center growth also corroborates a cycle while showing aggressive competitors. Passives, SiC and automotive sockets have different margins; no universal AI scarcity premium is assigned. [ST primary current results](https://newsroom.st.com/media-center/press-item.html/stmicroelectronics-reports-q3-2026-financial-results.html).

**Evidence gap:** Current product-level market share, contract margins and complete peer earnings revisions are not all disclosed or independently comparable. Competitive observations support the mechanism, but do not establish monopoly pricing or the exact terminal growth assumed.
