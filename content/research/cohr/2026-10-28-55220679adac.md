## Investment thesis

Coherent supplies optical components, transceivers and photonic technology used in data centers and industrial systems. Faster AI networks can raise sales of advanced optical products, while manufacturing integration can capture more value per connection. The latest fiscal quarter supports the growth mechanism, but factory spending, capital claims and cash conversion determine the benefit per diluted unit. The October 28 assessment remains EXIT because the $245 closing risk line has failed and the rebound remains below it. The repaired model has positive expected price return, but its roughly 21% weighted upside is below the framework's first-starter hurdle and does not reset the failed setup. The strongest opposing view is that the optical ramp sustains margins and growth above base. That needs customer production revenue and cash evidence rather than announcement momentum.

**Research date October 28, 2026.** Completed-session quote **$239.59**; independent comparison **$239.62**, difference 0.013%. After-hours prices are excluded from closing triggers. Model date October 28; terminal date 2028-06-30.

## How the business creates value

Data-center operators require reliable high-speed optical links; Coherent supplies devices and subsystems that help meet bandwidth and power requirements. In-house materials and component capabilities may improve cost and supply control, but also require factory investment and yield execution. Industrial laser and materials demand follow different cycles from AI networking, so a single AI multiple should not be applied to every segment without considering mix. The FY2028 base assumes $14 billion sales and an 18% adjusted net margin, well above fiscal2026 revenue, requiring both data-center growth and margin conversion. New optical architectures can increase content while cannibalizing legacy designs; customer qualification and capital efficiency determine net benefit.

### Five macro channels

- **Rates 1/5:** Debt service, customer finance and terminal multiples face higher discount rates.
- **Inflation 1/5:** Materials, energy and manufacturing costs affect optical margins.
- **Liquidity 4/5:** Funding access matters because capacity investment precedes cash recovery.
- **Cycle 2/5:** AI capex and industrial demand create different cycles within the company.
- **Policy 1/5:** Export restrictions, supply geography and customer access affect volumes.

The resulting macro assessment is **9/25**, an uptrend under pressure. V5.2 applies a 50% cap on normal exposure and starter/add pacing in this band; it does not mechanically reduce intrinsic value or create a stock-selection veto. The October 28 Fed decision kept the policy range at 3.75–4.00%; the official ten-year Treasury par yield was 5.05%. High rates remain material even after the rebound in equity indices.
