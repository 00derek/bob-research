## Investment thesis

Coherent makes optical components, lasers and related materials used in data centers, communications and industrial applications. AI networking creates strong demand for faster optical connections, but factories must turn that demand into cash after inventory, equipment and financing. The October 31 assessment is **EXIT at $210.32**, the October 30 close. The previously declared **$330 closing-risk reference has failed**; a new forecast also gives **$164.47 weighted present value**, below the quote. A rebuilt model cannot quietly erase the failed setup by renaming the security WATCH. The strongest opposing case is that scarce indium-phosphide capacity, 1.6 T products and better factory utilization sustain faster revenue growth and margins than the base. The bull case models that path explicitly. [Full FY2026 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/820318/000082031826000020/iivi-20260630.htm).

## Business economics and growth

Coherent supplies components and transceivers that turn electrical signals into optical links. Materials expertise, manufacturing yield, customer qualification and integrated design can sustain a competitive position. Lumentum and other optical suppliers compete, while customers can diversify designs and pressure pricing. A factory capacity announcement creates neither a guaranteed sale nor a fixed margin: product mix, yields, optical architecture and qualification determine returns.

June-quarter revenue was **$2.046 billion versus $1.529 billion**; GAAP gross margin **38.5% versus 35.7%**, operating profit **$254 million versus $6 million** and common net income **$241 million versus a $96 million loss**. GAAP EPS was **$1.19 versus negative $0.83**; adjusted EPS **$1.74** is a different metric. The latest released quarter is June, not an assumed September result. [August 12 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/820318/000119312526346860/d128030dex991.htm).

The call describes strong data-center demand, rising 1.6 T volumes and investment in InP, co-packaged optics and optical circuit switching. Management expected to double relevant InP capacity and cited attractive project paybacks. Those are company expectations, with execution risk. First-quarter FY2027 guidance was **$2.2–2.4 billion revenue**, **39.5–41.5% adjusted gross margin**, **$400–420 million adjusted operating expenses**, **18–20% tax** and **$1.85–2.05 adjusted EPS**. The model does not label those adjusted margins and EPS as GAAP common earnings. [Full August call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cohr/transcripts/661756-q4-2026/).

The June NIST announcement described a **letter of intent for up to $50 million** for InP capacity, not $50 million of cash already received. Public support can improve project economics but does not remove matching investment, eligibility and delivery conditions. **Evidence gap:** Current independently verified market share, comparable manufacturing yield and a synchronized optical-industry revisions panel were not established. No industry-flow or revision points are fabricated. [NIST primary announcement](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/06/department-commerces-chips-program-announces-letter-intent-coherent-50).

Lumentum's October 6 primary annual letter reports that1.6 T module production had started and its optical circuit-switch business exceeded a $100 million quarterly run rate. This is concrete competing supply in the same technology transitions, rather than a reason to assume Coherent has exclusive access to AI demand. Rival claims about future billions and manufacturing leadership remain management expectations, not independent market-share evidence. [Lumentum primary annual letter, page2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633978/000130817926000422/lite-ars.pdf).

### Cash and funding feasibility

The base $12 billion terminal revenue at **40.5% gross margin** generates **$4.86 billion gross profit**. Cash-equivalent operating expenses of **$2.54 billion** leave **$2.32 billion EBIT**. Deducting **$200 million net financing**, applying **20% tax**, and allowing approximately **$16 million outside minority profit** gives **$1.68 billion common earnings**, matching the 14% net margin. The path requires meaningful growth from current guidance, not retention of disposal gains or a 7% tax rate.

An illustrative FY2027 budget uses **$10.6 billion revenue/11% net margin**, adds **$700 million depreciation**, deducts **$900 million working capital**, and spends **$2.4 billion capex**, producing **negative $1.434 billion FCF**. Against $1.987 billion unrestricted consolidated liquidity this leaves approximately **$553 million**, before other demands. FY2028's $11.5 billion sales/13% net margin, $780 million depreciation, $300 million working capital and $1.5 billion capex generate **$475 million FCF**. FY2029 adds $800 million depreciation and deducts $500 million working capital and $900 million capex from $1.68 billion earnings, yielding **$1.08 billion FCF**; $400 million debt repayment leaves $680 million retained.

This budget assumes capex peaks while new capacity is installed, then declines as growth moderates. Current quarterly capex is accelerating: Q4 spent **$556 million**, versus $290 million in Q3, and management expected Q1 higher. If FY2027 working-capital demand is **$1.8 billion instead of $900 million**, the budget requires at least **$347 million additional funding**. Keeping FY2029 capex at $2.4 billion would consume $1.5 billion more than the base, eliminating its free-cash headroom. This is a conditional feasibility test, not an assurance of finance, and is why strong demand does not earn a full cash score.

At the same terminal date, discount and bull 32-times multiple, the quote requires **$8.8888 diluted EPS**, below bull's **$12.2927**: reverse gate **PASS**. A secondary forecast lists **26 rating analysts**, mean target **$392.40**, fiscal 2027 revenue **$10.6 billion** and **$9.40 adjusted EPS**, financial estimates dated October 30. Those adjusted near-term estimates are not the model's later normalized common EPS. **Evidence gap:** Future GAAP margins, capex normalization, working capital and competitive pricing remain assumptions.
