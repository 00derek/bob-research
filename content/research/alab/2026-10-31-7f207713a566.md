## Investment thesis

Astera Labs supplies chips and software that keep data moving among processors, memory and accelerators. Faster links and larger AI clusters can increase its content per system; qualification and diagnostics can make a successful design difficult to replace. The October 31 security assessment is **AVOID at $258.70**, the October 30 close. A newly rebuilt model gives **$165.63 weighted present value**, approximately **36.0% downside**, despite assuming substantial further sales growth. The strongest opposing case is that Scorpio switches and optical products make Astera a broader infrastructure supplier with much more content per accelerator. The bull tests that expansion, while the base normalizes taxes, compensation and the multiple. Announced platforms are future opportunities rather than current revenue. [Full June statements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1736297/000173629726000035/alab-20260630.htm).

## Business economics and competitive demands

Aries conditions PCIe signals, Scorpio switches connect compute fabrics, Taurus addresses Ethernet links and Leo supports memory connectivity. Customers value reliable links because unstable interconnects can leave expensive processors idle. COSMOS monitoring and the qualification process help integration, but standards, hyperscaler bargaining power and competing silicon limit the assumption of unlimited pricing power. Broadcom, Marvell, integrated platform designs and customer-developed connectivity can compete for content. Larger systems can increase both opportunity and design complexity.

June-quarter sales were **$392.400 million**, versus **$191.925 million** a year earlier and **$308.361 million** in March. Gross profit was **$287.567 million**, operating income **$89.248 million**, and pretax income **$102.825 million**. Net income of **$153.088 million** exceeds pretax income because of a **$50.263 million tax benefit**, chiefly related to equity-compensation tax deductions. The reported **$0.83 GAAP EPS** should not be annualized with that benefit intact. At an illustrative 18% normalized tax on pretax profit, quarterly EPS would be approximately **$0.46** on 183.340 million diluted shares. This is a normalization, not an alternative reported result. [Income statement and tax note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1736297/000173629726000035/alab-20260630.htm).

The August 4 call says PCIe 6 products contributed over half of revenue and Scorpio was expected to become the largest product family in Q3, one quarter earlier than previously expected. Guidance is **$540–560 million Q3 revenue**, **72% GAAP gross margin**, **$232–236 million GAAP operating expenses**, and **$0.87–0.92 GAAP EPS** on about **185 million diluted shares**. The unusually low **4% guided tax rate** and $76 million quarterly stock-compensation exclusion in adjusted expenses are material differences from recurring economic earnings. Scorpio customer expansion and production shipments must support this acceleration; Q3 financial results have not yet been released. [Issuer release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1736297/000173629726000033/q226exhibit991.htm); [full management call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/alab/transcripts/660931-q2-2026/).

October product announcements broaden the opportunity without proving sales. Aries 7 is expected to begin sampling in Q4, with copper and optical options and customer qualification still ahead. Orion's rack reference designs are expected through OEM/ODM partners starting Q1 2027; **Astera does not plan to manufacture complete racks itself**. These distinctions prevent valuing rack hardware revenue as though it all belongs to Astera. [October 8 Aries announcement](https://ir.asteralabs.com/node/9336/pdf); [October 12 Orion announcement and FAQ](https://www.asteralabs.com/news/astera-labs-unveils-orion-a-compute-agnostic-rack-scale-architecture-for-token-efficient-ai-factories/).

**Evidence gap:** Independent product-share and peer-utilization tables were not verified. Direct-customer concentration was **29%/25%/15%/13%** for the largest four, but several buyers are manufacturing partners; changing routing can alter these percentages without changing end-customer demand. It would be wrong to equate those figures to four identified hyperscaler exposures. Future product availability is also not a shipment commitment.

## Financial health and shareholder claims

First-half operating cash was **$162.276 million**, versus **$145.870 million**, while PPE cash spending rose from **$6.562 million to $28.054 million**. CFO less capex fell from **$139.308 million to $134.222 million** despite doubled sales. The cash reconciliation includes **$112.905 million stock compensation** and **$12.313 million customer-warrant contra-revenue**, while receivables and inventory consumed cash. Those noncash adjustments should not be treated as permanently costless. Receivables of **$192.469 million** and inventory **$113.781 million** roughly doubled from year-end, requiring monitoring as production ramps. [Cash flow and working capital](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1736297/000173629726000035/alab-20260630.htm).

Cash and marketable securities total **$1.253 billion**, with no financial debt identified in the balance sheet. Vendor statistics sometimes label operating leases as debt; approximately **$44.41 million** of lease obligations should be distinguished from borrowing. The company has purchase commitments of approximately **$181.669 million**, including software/cloud and supply commitments. No preferred or material minority claim was identified. Cash and positive operating income support a quality-gate **PASS**, but valuation still needs a realistic share denominator and tax rate.

June common shares were **173.485 million**, with **9.180 million RSUs**, **375,000 PSUs**, **1.693 million options** and maximum customer warrants totaling **5.578 million**. The fully expanded gross claim is therefore approximately **190.311 million shares** before forfeitures or treasury-stock proceeds. The February warrant alone covers **3.262 million shares at $142.82**, contingent on purchases, and had **$280 million grant-date value**. Old and new warrants reduce reported customer revenue as earned; their economic effect cannot simply disappear because headline gross margin remains high.

The new model fixes **190.311 million shares in all cases**, including the maximum existing award/warrant claims. It assumes cash-equivalent compensation for future services in forecast margins, with no additional future award dilution and no stock-compensation addback. Existing warrant claims are captured in that denominator: forward normalized revenue is measured after cash customer discounts and excludes amortization of those already-counted legacy warrants. No option/warrant exercise proceeds or surplus-cash premium is added. This avoids claiming both a cash windfall and an undiluted share count; it also avoids a second speculative future-award dilution penalty.

**Evidence gap:** Actual future vesting, forfeiture, settlement prices, cash incentives and acquisition funding remain unknown. Existing unrecognized compensation is substantial, and the normalized compensation convention is different from a literal GAAP earnings projection. Results must be reconciled to that convention at the next filing, rather than silently assuming away compensation costs.

## Rebuilt per-share valuation

The October 31 model ends **December 31, 2028**, **2.1684 ACT/365.25 years** away, discounted at **14%**. Base sales of **$4 billion** require about **44.7% annual growth** from the approximately $1.91 billion 2026 forecast, with Scorpio, higher content and new customers contributing. Base net margin is **26%**, after normalized 18% tax and economic compensation, compared with approximately 22.7% reported June operating margin and the 42.9% Q3 adjusted operating-margin guide. Bull requires **$6 billion sales and 34% net margin**; bear permits **$2.8 billion sales and 20% margin** despite continued growth. These paths test how a strong company can still have an overvalued stock.
