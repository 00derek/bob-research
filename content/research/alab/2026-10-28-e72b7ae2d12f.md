## Investment thesis

Astera Labs supplies connectivity products that keep AI systems communicating reliably as data rates and system complexity rise. Its retimers and fabric switches can gain content as customers build larger computing clusters. The latest quarter and Q3 guidance show a sharp revenue ramp, but the price already discounts a large share of the modeled success. The October 28 assessment remains AVOID because the rebuilt weighted value is well below the completed-session quote and no structural entry map is qualified. This review also corrects a forward/reverse mismatch and removes an unjustified extra cash addition from the old model. The strongest opposing case is that Scorpio switches and new optical or custom products create a broader earnings platform than the base case captures. That possibility needs production revenue, customer diversity and cash evidence, not just design wins.

**Research date October 28, 2026.** Completed-session quote **$297.20**; independent comparison **$297.39**, difference 0.064%. After-hours prices are excluded from closing triggers. Model date October 28; terminal date 2028-12-31.

## How the business creates value

Signal-conditioning products address the electrical difficulty of moving high-speed data across boards and cables; fabric switches address communication within larger AI systems. Customers value reliability, interoperability and power efficiency, while design cycles and qualification create switching friction. Growth is limited by customer deployments, manufacturing availability and product execution rather than an unlimited addressable-market estimate. The base case needs $3 billion FY2028 revenue, compared with Q3 guidance near $550 million per quarter, and a 28% sustainable net margin. New products may expand content but can also shift demand between existing products and require higher engineering costs. Customer concentration and the response of larger connectivity vendors remain material.

### Five macro channels

- **Rates 1/5:** High required returns penalize distant earnings and premium multiples.
- **Inflation 1/5:** Foundry, packaging and engineering costs can reduce incremental margins.
- **Liquidity 4/5:** Customer financing and risk appetite affect deployment timing and equity multiples.
- **Cycle 2/5:** Hyperscaler capex drives orders; a secular need does not remove quarterly cycles.
- **Policy 1/5:** Export restrictions and supply-chain rules can narrow customer access.

The resulting macro assessment is **9/25**, an uptrend under pressure. V5.2 applies a 50% cap on normal exposure and starter/add pacing in this band; it does not mechanically reduce intrinsic value or create a stock-selection veto. The October 28 Fed decision kept the policy range at 3.75–4.00%; the official ten-year Treasury par yield was 5.05%. High rates remain material even after the rebound in equity indices.

**Evidence gap:** A complete product/customer concentration schedule, competitor order comparison and synchronized industry-wide estimate-revision history were not established. These omissions limit confidence in the forecast bridge and industry score; the next filing and peer results should test them.
