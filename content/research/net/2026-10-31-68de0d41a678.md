## Investment thesis

Cloudflare sells security, network performance and developer infrastructure through a shared global network. A wider network and developer ecosystem can support product adoption while letting customers replace several vendors. AI agents may create new machine traffic and application workloads, adding to that opportunity. The price already requires a large part of the operating success: at $280.70 on October 30, the conditional weighted estimate is $224.07, about 20% below spot. The assessment is **Trim**, with the price above 115% of modeled value. The central dependency is that rapid sales growth ultimately becomes common earnings after compensation, network investment and dilution. The strongest opposing case is that agent infrastructure accelerates growth and operating leverage beyond the base assumptions. That is possible, but developer counts and product launches cannot be substituted for paid revenue and cash conversion.

## How the business creates value

Security subscriptions, content delivery and Workers computing share infrastructure and customer relationships. The network can improve response times and threat visibility, creating reasons to bundle products. Hyperscaler platforms, security vendors and do-it-yourself infrastructure remain alternatives. More usage can increase value but also network cost, so growth must improve recurring gross profit and normalized earnings.

The [August 6 Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477333/000147733326000053/q226exhibit991.htm) reports $696.1 million revenue, up 36%, 71.8% GAAP gross margin, a $205.7 million operating loss and $56.4 million free cash flow. Adjusted operating income was $96.1 million, showing why GAAP and adjusted profitability must be distinguished. Management's FY2026 sales guide is $2.864–2.870 billion; diluted guidance uses about 374 million units. Cash and securities $4.163 billion coexist with convertible claims and compensation-related dilution.

The base FY2030 $9 billion revenue case requires about 33% annual growth from the FY2026 guide and 25% normalized net margin, versus today's GAAP operating loss. The 400 million terminal diluted-unit assumption allows growth from the current guidance count but is not a guarantee that every conversion outcome fits. The old $2.175 billion zero-coupon financing remains a sensitivity. **Evidence gap:** A complete GAAP-to-normalized common-earnings, conversion, cash/debt and network reinvestment schedule is incomplete. These gaps justify only 2/4 valuation-input confidence.

## Macro transmission

Rates materially change the discount on earnings four years away and customers' software budgets. Inflation affects engineering and network cost. Liquidity can support capital access and high-duration multiples but cannot generate recurring cash. Security demand is comparatively resilient, while consumption and enterprise sales remain cyclical. Data-localization, cybersecurity and AI policy can create both demand and compliance expense.

The October 31 shared macro assessment is **8/25**: rates 1/5, inflation 1/5, liquidity 2/5, cycle 3/5 and policy 1/5. September PCE released October 29 showed 3.6% headline and 3.1% core annual inflation, while third-quarter real GDP grew at a 3.4% annual rate. Persistent inflation and growth leave long-duration earnings exposed to discount-rate pressure. The 50% normal-size cap and starter/add-only limit remain; macro modifies size rather than company conviction.
