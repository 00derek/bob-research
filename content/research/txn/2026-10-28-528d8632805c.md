## Investment thesis

Texas Instruments sells analog and embedded chips used in industrial, automotive and other equipment. Long product lives and design integration can support durable margins; more 300 mm manufacturing should reduce unit costs when capacity is utilized. October 21 results show the recovery, but TRIM at $309.83 on October 28 follows valuation: price now exceeds 115% of the retained $254.36 weighted FY2027 estimate. Better operations do not guarantee attractive returns when the quote assumes much of the recovery. The key dependencies are utilization, sustainable margins and cash after investment/distributions. The strongest opposing case is data-center and industrial demand making normalized earnings materially higher than modeled. That needs supported sales or cash earnings, rather than a multiple automatically raised after a rally.

## How the business creates value

Analog devices manage power and real-world signals; embedded chips control equipment. Long-lived designs and a broad catalog support repeat sales and switching costs. Owned manufacturing can improve costs/supply but creates fixed investment, depreciation and utilization risk. Data-center content is an opportunity, while industrial/auto demand still determines much of the cycle.

October 21 Q3 evidence reports $6.055 billion revenue, $2.425 billion net income and $2.62 diluted EPS, versus Q2 revenue $5.463 billion and EPS $2.14. Q4 guide is $5.8–6.3 billion sales and $2.45–2.79 EPS. Trailing operating cash flow was $9.896 billion; issuer free cash flow $8.422 billion. That free-cash definition includes CHIPS incentive proceeds, which must not be counted twice in margin or valuation. The September balance sheet reports $8.142 billion cash/investments and $13.555 billion debt including its current portion. Q3 diluted units were 921 million; modeled 925–930 million denominators allow some dilution. Earnings must service debt before belonging to equity. [Primary evidence](https://investor.ti.com/static-files/8e80f7d1-cc0c-4cea-8295-d439990cafc8).

The October 23 post-Q3 model uses FY2027 base $26 billion revenue, 38% net margin and 925 million diluted units. Sales are 7% above annualized Q3, with analyst-normalized profit. Bear $21 billion/31% stresses utilization; bull $28.5 billion/38.95% requires strong broad conversion. Net income is after debt cost, so the earnings multiple does not subtract debt a second time. A new quote changes returns and sell-ladder eligibility, not these forecasts.

## What could break the thesis

Industrial/auto weakness can leave new factories underutilized and reduce returns on investment. Dividends, capex and debt compete for cash even with growing earnings. Structural loss of competitiveness or repeated inability to convert recovery demand into cash would invalidate the thesis beyond routine cycle volatility. The favorable contrary case is a more durable, profitable recovery than the base assumes.

Thesis **INTACT**; credibility **PASS**; GAAP pre-profit **No**; binary-event risk **MEDIUM**. The 15% plausible overnight gap is an analyst stress, not a forecast bound. A daily-close stop cannot guarantee execution at its level. For HIGH binary risk, the larger of stop distance and gap stress governs sizing.

**Evidence gap:** Complete current gap distributions, material capital claims and synchronized insider/short evidence are unavailable. Test the specific business failures above and refresh immediately on results, financing, governance or thesis-changing news.
