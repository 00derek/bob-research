## Investment thesis

Texas Instruments converts a broad range of analog and embedded products into long-lived customer revenue, with lower-cost 300 mm manufacturing supporting returns as factories fill. The October 21 quarter and above-seasonal Q4 outlook show that recovery arriving. This new model nevertheless values common earnings after acquisition funding, taxes and dilution; its discounted weighted estimate is materially below the October 30 price. TRIM is the security-level valuation consequence above 115% of fair value, despite healthy operating momentum. The favorable case is stronger industrial and data-center content with sustained pricing. The opposing case is that a cyclical peak and a higher financing burden are capitalized as permanent economics.

## How the business creates value

Power management and signal-chain chips process and control real-world electrical functions; embedded processors handle specialized tasks. Many customer programs last years, making product breadth, reliability and direct distribution valuable. Owning factories can create a cost advantage but leaves depreciation and cash commitments when demand softens. TI estimates chips made on 300 mm wafers cost roughly 40% less than on 200 mm; that is a production claim, not a guaranteed 40% corporate margin improvement. [Manufacturing model and segment discussion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/97476/000009747626000169/txn-20260930.htm).

STMicroelectronics October 29 results show US$3.76 billion revenue, up 17.9%, 37.3% gross margin and a stronger Q4 outlook. Different product mix and accounting prevent equal-margin ranking, but it confirms a broad recovery rather than a TI-only demand phenomenon. ST, Analog Devices and domestic Chinese analog competitors can contest sockets and pricing; the model gives no monopoly valuation. [ST primary Q3 results](https://newsroom.st.com/media-center/press-item.html/stmicroelectronics-reports-q3-2026-financial-results.html).

**Evidence gap:** Current product-level market share, contract margins and complete peer earnings revisions are not all disclosed or independently comparable. Competitive observations support the mechanism, but do not establish monopoly pricing or the exact terminal growth assumed.

## What could break the thesis

Industrial restocking and stronger lead times can reverse. China competitors, inventory obsolescence, factory utilization and acquisition integration can pressure both growth and margins. A high cash dividend reduces funding flexibility. Watch end-market sequential growth, book-to-bill commentary, inventory days, after-capex cash excluding subsidies and acquisition leverage. Persistent loading or pricing misses invalidate the base earnings margin.
