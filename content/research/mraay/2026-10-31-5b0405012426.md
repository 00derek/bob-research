## Investment thesis

Murata's latest results support stronger demand for high-value capacitors used in AI servers. They also reveal a large reinvestment commitment that an earnings-only valuation can understate. The security assessment is **TRIM** at the October 30 ADR close of $19.73, against newly rebuilt probability-weighted value of $13.10. The strong opposing case is that Murata's manufacturing know-how and rising capacitor content let revenue and cash margins compound fast enough to justify the $26.88 bull value. That outcome is plausible, but requires more than the current profit upgrade: the new factories must earn adequate returns after equipment, working capital, leases and currency translation.

## How the business creates value

Murata makes capacitors, inductors, filters, modules and other electronic components. Miniaturization, materials expertise, process yield and customer qualification create barriers, especially in advanced multilayer ceramic capacitors. AI servers increase demand for high-capacitance parts and stable power delivery. Smartphones, vehicles and other electronics remain important customers; AI strength does not automatically eliminate handset cyclicality or losses elsewhere.

## Macro transmission

Rates affect electronics customers' financing and the discount on future cash. Inflation raises materials and factory costs; liquidity influences the AI buildout; the cycle drives smartphone, automotive and industrial demand. Policy can alter customer geography and supply chains. Currency is directly important: the company assumes 155 JPY/USD for the second half and estimates a one-yen exchange movement changes annual sales about 9 billion and operating profit 4.5 billion. A stronger yen can reduce exporter earnings while increasing the dollar value of yen cash; the net effect is not captured by simply changing ADR translation alone.

Macro is **8/25: rates 1, inflation 1, liquidity 2, cycle 3, policy 1**, with 50% normal-size cap and starter/add-only increases. The [September PCE release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-september-2026) showed 3.6%/3.1% headline/core annual inflation; [Q 3 GDP](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-3rd-quarter-2026) grew 3.4% annualized. The model therefore keeps meaningful required returns and scenario-specific currency, rather than treating a favorable demand cycle as permanent cheap funding.

## What could break the thesis

The main failure mode is expansion that creates volume but disappointing cash returns. Warning signs include advanced-component pricing pressure, weaker utilization, persistent receivable/inventory absorption, or capital spending that fails to normalize as new buildings fill. A continued loss in devices/modules could offset the attractive MLCC mix. Customer concentration, handset weakness, auto demand and export policy remain relevant outside the AI narrative.

Credibility is **PASS** on reviewed evidence; no unsupported accounting allegation is inferred from volatility. The downside model is severe because a weaker cycle and heavy reinvestment can jointly compress equity value. The 20% gap stress is a planning scenario and not a loss limit. Binary event risk is MEDIUM; the next reported quarter will test the upgraded guide and working-capital normalization.
