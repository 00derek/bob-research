## Investment thesis

MercadoLibre combines a marketplace, delivery network, payments, advertising and consumer credit across Latin America. Better delivery and payment acceptance can attract more buyers and merchants, creating additional transactions and more reasons to stay within the ecosystem. That is a credible competitive loop, but growth benefits each common unit only after shipping subsidies, credit losses and investment. The August Q2 release shows revenue growing much faster than profit, so the thesis needs recovery in unit economics rather than perpetual volume expansion. At $1,673.09 on October 28, the $2,135.20 conditional weighted estimate offers about 28% upside. The assessment is **Watch** because the completed close is above the predeclared $1,500–1,640 starter zone. The strongest opposing case is that competition forces lasting subsidies while credit growth adds risk. A pullback into a still-valid setup or evidence of better cash conversion could change the assessment.

## How the business creates value

Merchants buy access to a large buyer base, fulfillment and advertising; consumers gain selection, delivery and payment convenience. Mercado Pago expands the addressable market and can deepen retention, while credit can both enable transactions and create losses. Brazil, Mexico and Argentina have different currencies, regulations and credit cycles. Logistics scale and payments density can create defensibility, but aggressive competitors can force reinvestment of that advantage.

The [August 5 Q2 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1099590/000109959026000021/meli-20260805xex991.htm) reports $10.169 billion revenue and financial income, up 50%, $683 million operating income and $466 million net income. Operating margin was6.7%; investment in shipping and fintech restrained profit. H1 operating cash of $5.737 billion includes customer funding, whereas issuer-adjusted free cash flow was only $158 million after restricted funds, capex and credit investment. Gross financial cash inflow is therefore not freely distributable shareholder cash.

The base $65 billion FY2028 revenue case is about 60% above annualized Q2 sales and requires6% common net margin. That is an analyst operating path, not issuer guidance. The model uses51 million diluted units, with higher dilution in the bear case. Restricted customer cash is not added as surplus equity value. **Evidence gap:** A current country/product margin bridge, credit-vintage performance and fully reconciled customer-funds/capital schedule are incomplete; the next filing must test the assumed earnings conversion.

## Macro transmission

Rates affect consumer credit affordability and loan funding costs. Inflation changes fulfillment expense, purchasing power and reported currency growth. Liquidity can support credit and seller working capital but can reverse during stress. Retail and credit are cyclical despite secular online penetration. Local financial regulation, sovereign risk and cross-border trade policy constrain resilience.

The October 28 shared macro assessment is **9/25**: rates 1/5, inflation 1/5, liquidity 4/5, cycle 2/5 and policy 1/5. The Fed maintained its policy range at 3.75–4.00%; the official 10-year Treasury par yield was 5.05%. This remains an uptrend under pressure with a 50% normal-risk sizing band and starter/add stages only. Macro changes permitted risk, rather than mechanically adding to company conviction or imposing a second market-direction multiplier. Brent observations conflict by contract/clock, and October 29 GDP/PCE were not yet released.

**Evidence gap:** The five economic channels above are qualitative sensitivities; no verified company-specific elasticity to rates, energy or liquidity is claimed. Quantify those effects in the operating scenarios if they materially change margins, financing or demand.

## What could break the thesis

The strongest opposing case is structurally lower take-home economics: competitors sustain shipping subsidies and customers take credit faster than risk-adjusted margins improve. Watch operating income against revenue growth, mature credit-vintage losses, restricted-cash movements and country-level contribution. FX and sovereign shocks can reduce dollar earnings even when local transaction growth is healthy. More durable margin compression or financing beyond the bear dilution would force a lower model. The 18% overnight-gap assumption is a stress test; it does not make a daily-close stop certain.
