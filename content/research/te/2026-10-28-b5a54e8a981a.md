## Investment thesis

T1 Energy is building U.S. solar manufacturing capacity, with module output and the G2 Austin cell project intended to capture domestic demand and manufacturing incentives. A favorable trade decision can improve input access, but shareholder value depends on funding factories and earning margins after financing and dilution. AVOID at $2.63 on October 28 follows failed business quality and an incomplete current financing/claims model. The October 22 polysilicon waiver is operating evidence, not a cure for those restrictions. The strongest opposing case is that domestic procurement and credits support profitable scale and attract favorable funding. That needs a binding funding package, adequate unrestricted cash, control remediation and realized manufacturing economics.

## How the business creates value

Module assembly converts purchased cells into panels; domestic cell production would move more value creation into the United States. It could capture policy support and reduce import dependence, while requiring fixed investment, supplier commitments and manufacturing yield. Solar demand does not ensure attractive panel margins in a competitive market. Announced capacity is not saleable output or collected cash.

The Q2 issuer release reported $156.4 million cash, equivalents and restricted cash, including only $79.1 million unrestricted cash. The October 22 waiver release continues to disclose the need for comprehensive financing for G2 Austin Phase 1 and a material financial-reporting control weakness. Freely available cash and committed construction costs must be modeled together; headline cash overstates liquidity if restrictions are ignored. [Primary evidence](https://ir.t1energy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/t1-energy-reports-second-quarter-2026-results).

Current valuation needs cost-to-complete, financing terms, interest, customer deposits, tax-credit eligibility/monetization, working capital and dilution. The old 450 million-unit terminal denominator is not recertified. Assuming funded growth without naming who supplies capital could assign common equity returns that belong to financing providers.
