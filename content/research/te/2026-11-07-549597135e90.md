## Investment thesis

T1 Energy manufactures solar modules in the United States and is building domestic cell capacity to improve local supply and capture incentives. At the **$2.36 November 6 completed close**, the November 7 re-underwritten model gives **$2.42 probability-weighted present value (+2.5%)**. The security assessment is **AVOID**. ## How the business creates value

Utility customers buy modules for power projects; domestic cells can improve supply security and eligibility for incentives. G1 Dallas assembles modules using purchased cells, while G2 Austin adds a more complex upstream process. The 5 GW module plant and planned 2.1 GW first cell phase are different capacities, not additive module sales. Q2 production 935 MW is an actual operating milestone. The current sales path is highly concentrated: the June filing reports one customer representing approximately 100% of sales and receivables. A transition toward independent customer contracts would improve quality only when cash collections demonstrate diversification.

First Solar's October 29 release reports $1.23 billion quarterly revenue, $1.6 billion gross cash and 44 GW backlog, while reducing 2026 sales guidance to $4.6–4.9 billion. Its thin-film technology differs from T1's crystalline silicon. The useful comparison is financial capacity and dependence on 45X: even a scaled competitor adjusts schedules and relies heavily on credits. T1 has less cash protection and concentrated customers, so domestic policy support alone does not establish a moat. [First Solar latest results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1274494/000127449426000211/ex991pressreleaseq3-2026.htm).

**Evidence gap:** Current product-level market share, contract margins and complete peer earnings revisions are not all disclosed or independently comparable. Competitive observations support the mechanism, but do not establish monopoly pricing or the exact terminal growth assumed.

October 30 executed offtake agreements cover more than 1.58 GW and lift contracted 2027 module capacity above 4 GW. The issuer expects a blend of imported and G2 domestic cells; first phase 2.1 GW cell production remains a Q1 2027 plan. Those contracts improve commercial visibility but do not disclose margins, collections, financing, or remove policy/control restrictions. The base still needs $450 million modeled external capital and heavy common dilution. Offtake is not construction cash. [Primary evidence](https://ir.t1energy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/t1-energy-signs-new-offtake-deals).
