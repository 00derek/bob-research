## Investment thesis

At $213.03 the October 28 model gives 10.31% upside, below the starter threshold, and there is no verified structural stop. WATCH persists despite the lower quote; stronger data-center sales need to convert to cash after acquisition spending.

TE Connectivity makes connectors and sensors that move data and power through vehicles, industrial equipment and data centers. More electrical content and faster links can increase sales even when equipment units grow slowly. Qualification and engineering integration support margins, while cash after investment determines the benefit per equity unit. October 28 results strengthen that mechanism. The important assumptions are order conversion, margin durability and disciplined acquisition/buyback spending. The strongest opposing case is that AI data/power demand makes the base forecast too modest. A larger opportunity must still convert through working capital, reinvestment and dilution to per-unit earnings.

## How the business creates value

Industrial products serve data centers, power infrastructure and factories; transportation products serve more complex electrical systems. Qualification can make a connector difficult to replace after a design win, but production and project schedules affect demand. Optical/power acquisitions broaden products while adding integration and funding demands. More content must create profitable sales rather than only a larger market forecast.

October 28 FY2026 results reported $19.885 billion sales versus $17.262 billion in FY2025, and Q4 sales $5.312 billion. Full-year operating cash flow was $4.5 billion and free cash flow $3.4 billion. Q4 orders were $6.1 billion; adjusted EPS $3.14 versus GAAP continuing EPS $3.10. Management guided FY2027 Q1 sales near $5.35 billion and adjusted EPS $3.30. Acquisitions, tariff-refund presentation and adjustments require care before extrapolating margins.
