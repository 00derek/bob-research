## Investment thesis

Teradyne sells automated semiconductor and electronics test equipment and robotics. More complex AI chips and memory can require more testing, allowing demand to grow faster than chip units; robotics provides another source of sales. The October 27 result confirms strong activity, but WATCH at $313.24 on October 28 reflects a price above the updated weighted FY2028 estimate. The base case already requires sustained AI test intensity, high utilization and profitable equipment conversion. The strongest opposing case is that memory and integrated-system testing grow for longer and robotics broadens earnings enough to support the bull path. That needs a durable orders-to-earnings bridge. One strong quarter cannot remove the equipment cycle or supply a margin of safety at any price.

## How the business creates value

Semiconductor test depends on device complexity, test time, customer investment and competitive share. Product test covers other electronics; collaborative/mobile robotics has different buyers and cycles. Complex AI/memory devices can increase test content, but large customers can pause orders after expanding capacity. Long qualification relationships support differentiation without eliminating concentration or competitor risk.

October 27 Q3 revenue was $1.306 billion versus $769 million a year earlier and $1.329 billion in Q2. GAAP EPS was $2.17 and adjusted EPS $2.29. Semiconductor Test sales were $1.072 billion, Product Test $138 million and Robotics $96 million. Q4 revenue guide is $1.25–1.35 billion and GAAP EPS $1.88–2.18. This supports strong annual growth alongside sequential normalization; adjusted and GAAP earnings remain distinct. [Primary evidence](https://investors.teradyne.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/455/teradyne-reports-third-quarter-2026-results).

Fresh FY2028 base retains $7 billion sales, 27% net margin and 160 million diluted units. Sales are 34% above the annualized Q4 guide midpoint, requiring continued growth. Bear $5 billion/20% normalizes spending; bull $9 billion/30% needs broad AI/memory adoption and favorable mix. Re-underwriting after Q3 retains the cases because actual demand supports them; price action does not raise assumptions.

## What could break the thesis

AI test orders could peak before terminal earnings, and concentrated buyers can make shipments volatile. Robotics may not offset a chip downturn. Watch order cancellations, lost test share/content, margin pressure and cash lagging profit. The favorable contrary case is profitable diversification across memory, systems and robotics with broad customer investment.
