## Investment thesis

Palantir sells software that connects data to operational decisions for government agencies and businesses. Its Artificial Intelligence Platform can expand those deployments as customers bring AI into workflows; high software margins mean additional recurring revenue could produce substantial cash. The difficult question is how much of that success the price already assumes. At the October 28 close of $196.81, the stock costs more than this report's optimistic FY2028 case. AVOID therefore reflects the return available at the price, despite strong operating momentum. Sustained adoption, margin durability and dilution are the three assumptions carrying the valuation. The strongest opposing case is that growth lasts longer than the two-year forecast and creates earnings beyond the modeled bull case. That possibility needs an explicit longer operating forecast; a higher multiple alone would not establish it.

## How the business creates value

Government buyers use Gotham and related platforms to combine sensitive information and coordinate decisions; commercial buyers use Foundry and AIP to operationalize enterprise data and AI. Integration with consequential workflows can make replacement costly, but does not eliminate competition from internal systems, hyperscalers and other software vendors. Concentrated U.S. demand, public procurement timing and policy controversy remain risks to predictable conversion. This is primarily a software adoption and customer-expansion case, rather than a manufacturing capacity story.

The August 3 Q2 release reported $1.935 billion revenue, up 93% year over year, while the Q2 filing shows $912 million operating income versus $269 million a year earlier. First-half government/commercial revenue was 52%/48%. Those figures support operating leverage; they do not justify treating tax effects, investment gains or stock compensation as recurring owner earnings. The forecast normalizes net margin and explicitly expands diluted units in the bull case. [Primary evidence](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321655/000132165526000039/a2026q2ex991pressrelease.htm).

The base forecast requires $18 billion FY2028 revenue and a 38% after-tax margin, a substantial expansion from Q2's annualized $7.74 billion sales. It assumes customer expansion continues after early AI deployments and implementation costs do not absorb the operating leverage. The bull case needs $26 billion and 43%; it is not management guidance. This review corrects the older displayed values to the documented 12% discount over two years; the former arithmetic could not be reproduced from those inputs. No price increase was used to raise an operating assumption.

## What could break the thesis

AIP deployments could generate excitement before recurring production spending, while public budgets or procurement disputes delay conversion. Slower customer expansion, sustained dilution or a deterioration in normalized cash earnings would undermine the modeled per-unit growth. The contrary risk to AVOID is durable growth far beyond FY2028; the next result must show retention and conversion, rather than just a larger contract headline.
