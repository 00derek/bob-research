## Investment thesis

Expedia can earn from travel demand through consumer brands and partner distribution while spreading its technology and supplier network over more bookings. The November 7 assessment is **TRIM** at the **$259.24 November 6 completed close**. New cash underwriting gives **$227.18 weighted present value (-12.4%)**. Better operations and a lower quote have not established the 25% upside needed for a starter. There is no qualified entry band or new tactical stop.

The optimistic case is that partner distribution grows while direct consumer engagement lowers acquisition cost, producing $478.99 bull value. The opposing case is that AI intermediaries and hotel direct channels control customer acquisition, forcing more commissions and marketing spend just as travel demand slows. Bear value is $75.11. The crucial cash distinction is between cash earned after providing travel and cash temporarily advanced by customers.

## Business economics and financial evidence

Expedia, Hotels.com and Vrbo connect customers with lodging and other travel services. B2B supplies inventory, rates and technology to travel and non-travel partners. Merchant bookings collect cash before suppliers are paid; agency arrangements generally earn a commission. Gross bookings measure travel transaction value, while recognized revenue is the platform's own economics. Neither hotel prices nor customer advances are common profit. Supplier breadth, service, loyalty and transaction reliability support repeat use, but search engines, Booking, hotels and new AI planning tools can compete for the same customer.

The [November 4 quarterly release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1324424/000132442426000059/earningsrelease-q32026.htm) reports 115.7 million booked room nights, up 7%, $34.008 billion gross bookings, up 11%, and $4.938 billion revenue, up 12%. B2B revenue grew 23%. Operating income was $1.326 billion; common net income $1.030 billion and GAAP EPS $8.42. Q4 revenue guidance slows to $3.69–3.75 billion, up 4.0–5.7%, despite the raised full-year $16.37–16.43 billion guide. A strong peak-season quarter cannot be multiplied by four.

The current [September 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1324424/000132442426000062/expe-20260930.htm) explains the distribution tradeoff: direct marketing includes search acquisition and partner commissions. Higher B2B commissions helped raise marketing dollars even as consumer marketing gained leverage. AI can improve service cost and conversion or shift bargaining power to outside platforms; neither outcome is established by an integration announcement. [Booking's November 3 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000044/q3-26bkngearningsrelease.htm) also show 7% room-night growth and 10% gross-bookings growth. That supports broad travel demand, rather than proving Expedia gained share. Booking's legal adjustments prevent a simple comparison of reported revenue or margins.
