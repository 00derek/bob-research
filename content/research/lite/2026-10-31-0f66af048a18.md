## Investment thesis

Lumentum's optical products are becoming more important as AI networks move data faster and reach beyond copper's practical limits. The business is accelerating; the price still requires much of that growth to reach common shareholders. The rebuilt assessment is **TRIM** at the October30 close of$840.70, against **$540.57** probability-weighted value. This review resolves the preferred and convertible claims instead of treating adjusted EPS as if it belonged to an unchanged share count. The strongest opposing case is that capacity expansion and optical circuit switching produce the$1,144.84 bull value. A reported potential acquisition could also move price sharply, but no binding terms were verified and no takeover premium is embedded in the standalone model.

## How the business creates value

Lumentum supplies indium-phosphide lasers, optical modules, transceivers and optical circuit switches. Materials, yields, qualification, precision and reliability create barriers. Faster interconnects can increase content per network and open new in-rack uses. Large customers also have bargaining power, can qualify alternative suppliers and can change architectures. Coherent and other optical suppliers compete; co-packaged optics can create opportunities for lasers while replacing some existing module designs. Demand is not equivalent to company revenue until product capacity, yield and customer qualification deliver shipments.

The [August11 release](https://investor.lumentum.com/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Lumentum-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Fiscal-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx) reports fiscalQ4, ended June27, revenue$1.0063bn versus$0.8084bn sequentially and$0.4807bn a year earlier. GAAP operating margin27.8% compares with21.6% and−1.7%; adjusted operating margin36.6% compares with32.2% and15%. Adjusted EPS$3.23 was up from$2.37/$0.88. Full-year sales$3.014bn grew83.2% from$1.645bn. The GAAP annual loss$6.9351bn includes$7.7566bn noncash debt-extinguishment expense; it is not a cash operating burn of that amount. The common-equity dilution behind the accounting charge nevertheless matters.

The [actual fiscal2026 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633978/000162828026057358/lite-20260627.htm) shows operating cash$751.4m versus$126.3m, capex$451.3m versus$231.0m and stock-compensation cash-flow adjustment$170.2m. The [Q4 call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lite/transcripts/660925-q4-2026/) describes growing pump and narrow-linewidth lasers, new1.6T transceiver shipments, expanding Japanese InP capacity and a first expected triple-digit-million OCS quarter. ## What could break the thesis

The failure case is expanding optical demand that produces lower company returns because yields, qualification, product mix or pricing disappoint. More capacity at Lumentum and its rivals can eventually weaken scarcity. OCS concentration can magnify one customer's changes. Architectural shifts can move value from modules to lasers or to competing designs. Counterparty failure can weaken hedge protection; cash conversion requests can consume liquidity even when accounting earnings are strong.

Credibility is **PASS** on reviewed evidence. Binary risk is **HIGH** around the confirmed results and reported deal talks, with25% plausible-gap stress. That stress is not a loss limit. A binding merger agreement, material customer loss or changed capital terms would require a fresh valuation immediately.
