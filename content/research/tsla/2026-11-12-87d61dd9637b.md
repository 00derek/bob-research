## Investment thesis

Tesla combines vehicles, energy storage and software with proposed autonomous transport and robotics, but the current common value depends on cash returns from services that are not yet proven at the modeled scale. The **November 12, 2026 assessment is TRIM**, using the **$390.84 November 11 completed close**. The retained **November 7** model estimates **$257.30 probability-weighted present value (-34.2%)**, with **$21.23 bear** and **$745.21 bull** values. Price remains above 115% of weighted value, so the conditional 50% valuation target survives.

Fleet distribution and autonomous services could create high-margin recurring profit beyond a conventional manufacturer. Safety constraints, competitive pricing and physical investment could consume that benefit before it reaches common equity. A possible SpaceX combination receives no premium without binding terms; a price rebound does not establish autonomous-service profits.

**Evidence gap:** The operating forecasts, capital claims and discount dates remain those of the November 7 model after current issuer, filing and news reassessment. Current price, reverse requirements, timing and rule outcomes are recalculated; this is not a price-driven forecast upgrade. Named competitive, funding and event gaps remain next to their consequences.

## How the business creates value

### Current assessment and evidence

The October 21 result and September filing remain the latest retrieved operating basis. Independent committees are exploring a possible SpaceX relationship, with no binding exchange ratio established. The November 11 rebound changes valuation-reduction tests but does not raise the autonomy forecast, assume completed merger terms or make undrawn credit into cash. [Current primary basis](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000162828026066775/tsla-20260930.htm); [dated current check](https://ir.tesla.com/).

Vehicle buyers pay for range, charging, software and ownership experience; storage customers pay for capacity and reliability. Manufacturing scale and the installed fleet can spread research cost and distribute software at low incremental cost. That advantage is constrained by vehicle replacement cycles, price competition and the need to validate safety across new roads and regulators. Robotaxi revenue belongs within services today; a supervised driver-assistance subscription is different from a fully autonomous commercial ride. Optimus also needs productive customer deployments rather than a demonstration to establish unit economics.

Waymo's October 20 primary announcement reports more than 20 million completed trips and hundreds of thousands of commercial rides weekly, while planning initial Madrid riders in 2027. That confirms active autonomous competition without proving equal cost structures or comparing a geofenced ride to supervised software. Tesla's broad installed fleet could offer distribution advantages; competitors already demonstrate commercial service. [Waymo October update](https://www.waymo.com/blog/2026/10/waymo-in-madrid-region/).

**Evidence gap:** Current product-level market share, contract margins and complete peer earnings revisions are not all disclosed or independently comparable. Competitive observations support the mechanism, but do not establish monopoly pricing or the exact terminal growth assumed.

The September 10-Q still identifies independent-director exploration of a possible SpaceX relationship and explicitly warns no transaction is assured. No merger premium or assumed exchange ratio is included. The released quarter has flat sales, lower operating income and tariff-refund benefits; its current economics do not justify treating autonomy and robotics as already earned profit. The current completed quote remains above the conditional valuation-reduction boundary. [Primary evidence](https://ir.tesla.com/_flysystem/s3/sec/000162828026066775/tsla-20260930-gen.pdf).

Liquidity ends those years around $37.4/$35.4/$40.4/$53.4 billion before debt amortization and acquisitions; a $10 billion reserve for those uses still leaves positive cash. The base therefore does not need new equity proceeds to survive this path. Bear assumes $10 billion cumulative extra net borrowing and slower capital spending, with more dilution from compensation; bull reinvests more but funds it with higher cash flow. These are analyst budgets, not management commitments; new fab or combination obligations can invalidate them.

The terminal share bridge starts with 3.953 billion actual shares and adds 547 million net claims in the base case, or 747 million in bull. These totals are deliberately above the current issued count and cover ordinary employee awards, the economic cost of contingent CEO compensation and any financing/acquisition settlement. The model uses an all-in terminal denominator rather than adding every award twice. Base includes roughly 200 million ordinary net new claims and 347 million contingent/transaction/financing allowance; bull includes more earned awards with stronger performance. This is conservative economic dilution, not a claim that these exact additional units are legally outstanding. A cap-table update or a binding exchange ratio must replace the allowance.

The 200 million ordinary future-claim allowance can overlap with cash-equivalent compensation already charged to margins. Removing it gives 4.30 billion base units and base present value $213.26. This conservative sensitivity does not remove contingent-award economic cost or establish a current share count.

## What could break the thesis

Autonomy and robotics must become large, profitable products for the base to work. Lower software retention, unsafe deployment, costly claims, delayed approvals or price cuts can prevent fixed-cost leverage. The $786 million tariff recovery improves cash today while complicating margin extrapolation. Related-party investment and the possible combination create governance/conflict risk; independent committees do not establish fair exchange terms. Observable failures are persistent automotive margin weakness excluding credits/refunds, declining paid subscription retention, deployment restrictions and capex growing faster than operating cash generation.
