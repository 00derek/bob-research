## Investment thesis

Tesla sells electric vehicles, storage and software while building an autonomous transport and robotics business. At the **$358.22 November 6 completed close**, the November 7 re-underwritten model gives **$257.30 probability-weighted present value (-28.2%)**. The security assessment is **TRIM**. The completed close exceeds 115% of probability-weighted fair value, producing the framework’s conditional 50% valuation-reduction target; it remains below the modeled bull boundary. The strongest favorable case is that the installed vehicle base makes software and autonomous services scale much faster than a conventional manufacturer. The opposing case is that safety validation, regulatory limits, competition and physical investment consume the margin benefit before common shareholders receive it. The potential SpaceX combination is excluded until binding exchange terms exist.

**Evidence gap:** Future margins, capital needs and terminal multiples remain analyst estimates. The body distinguishes released financials, issuer plans and modeled economics; missing current consensus and event evidence prevent treating the estimate as a forecast guarantee.

## How the business creates value

Vehicle buyers pay for range, charging, software and ownership experience; storage customers pay for capacity and reliability. Manufacturing scale and the installed fleet can spread research cost and distribute software at low incremental cost. That advantage is constrained by vehicle replacement cycles, price competition and the need to validate safety across new roads and regulators. Robotaxi revenue belongs within services today; a supervised driver-assistance subscription is different from a fully autonomous commercial ride. Optimus also needs productive customer deployments rather than a demonstration to establish unit economics.

Waymo's October 20 primary announcement reports more than 20 million completed trips and hundreds of thousands of commercial rides weekly, while planning initial Madrid riders in 2027. That confirms active autonomous competition without proving equal cost structures or comparing a geofenced ride to supervised software. Tesla's broad installed fleet could offer distribution advantages; competitors already demonstrate commercial service. [Waymo October update](https://www.waymo.com/blog/2026/10/waymo-in-madrid-region/).

**Evidence gap:** Current product-level market share, contract margins and complete peer earnings revisions are not all disclosed or independently comparable. Competitive observations support the mechanism, but do not establish monopoly pricing or the exact terminal growth assumed.

The September 10-Q still identifies independent-director exploration of a possible SpaceX relationship and explicitly warns no transaction is assured. No merger premium or assumed exchange ratio is included. The released quarter has flat sales, lower operating income and tariff-refund benefits; its current economics do not justify treating autonomy and robotics as already earned profit. The lower November 6 price remains above the conditional valuation-reduction boundary. [Primary evidence](https://ir.tesla.com/_flysystem/s3/sec/000162828026066775/tsla-20260930-gen.pdf).
