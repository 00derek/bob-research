## Investment thesis

Arm earns licenses and royalties from processor technology and is adding its own CPU hardware business. Compatibility supports adoption, but hardware requires inventory and supplier commitments and cannot inherit the economics of near-zero-cost IP royalties. The **November 12, 2026 security assessment is EXIT**, using the **$203.36 November 11 completed close**. The favorable opposing case needs a larger, independently funded server/CPU business than these already demanding scenarios.

## Business economics and macro transmission

Licensing gives customers access to architectures or implementations; royalties monetize subsequent chip shipments. Software compatibility and developer skills encourage reuse, while x 86 and RISC-V provide alternatives. Arm's own CPU can capture more dollars per device but also compete with customers and require manufacturing capacity, inventory and a much lower gross margin than IP licensing. It is a change in economic mix, not free royalty revenue.

**Evidence gap:** Independent CPU shipment commitments, customer-level realized economics and synchronized industry revisions were not verified. A $2 billion demand pipeline is not $2 billion recognized revenue, and the 97% IP gross margin cannot be applied to all future CPU sales. The forecast separates those economic streams.
