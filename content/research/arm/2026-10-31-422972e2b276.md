## Investment thesis

Arm licenses processor architecture and designs, collects royalties as customers ship chips, and is now expanding into its own production CPU. Its software ecosystem and power efficiency support demand across phones, cloud and embedded systems. The October 31 security signal is **AVOID at $205.97**, the October 30 close. A fully rebuilt forecast gives **$81.13 weighted present value**, approximately **60.6% downside**. The quote also requires earnings beyond the identical bull case, producing a **BLOCK** on the reverse-valuation gate. The strongest opposing case is that server royalties and an owned CPU business make Arm much larger and more profitable. That is plausible business upside; it is already a demanding equity assumption when recurring compensation, lower hardware margins and controller-related revenue are recognized. [June quarterly filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1973239/000197323926000114/arm-20260630.htm).

## Business economics and the transition into silicon

Licensing gives customers access to architectures or implementations; royalties monetize subsequent chip shipments. Software compatibility and developer skills encourage reuse, while x86 and RISC-V provide alternatives. Arm's own CPU can capture more dollars per device but also compete with customers and require manufacturing capacity, inventory and a much lower gross margin than IP licensing. It is a change in economic mix, not free royalty revenue.

Fiscal Q1 2027 ended **June 30** and was reported **July 29**. Revenue rose from **$1.053 billion to $1.289 billion**, including **$715 million royalties** and **$574 million licensing**. Operating income, however, fell from **$114 million to $91 million**, a **7.1% GAAP operating margin**, as R&D reached **$838 million**. Reported net income of **$270 million** includes a **$128 million equity-investment gain**, plus **$17 million tax benefit**; those are not recurring operating profits. At a normalized 20% tax, excluding the investment mark but retaining ordinary financing income, indicative earnings are much lower than the headline net income. The model therefore starts from operating economics rather than annualizing $0.25 quarterly GAAP EPS.

Related parties contributed **$388 million**, roughly **30% of quarterly revenue**. SoftBank licensing/design services alone contributed **$193 million**, with management expecting approximately $200 million per quarter thereafter. Contract assets include substantial related-party amounts. These are disclosed customer relationships, not automatically improper revenue, but their independence, repeatability and cash collection matter when assessing growth quality. [Full revenue and related-party notes](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1973239/000197323926000114/arm-20260630.htm).

Management says AGI CPU demand exceeds **$2 billion across fiscal 2027/2028**, while manufacturing capacity is secured for the previously described **$1 billion opportunity**. Demand and capacity are different. Initial hardware gross margin was discussed in the high 30s/low 40s, with a potential path toward 50% over subsequent years. Q2 guide is **$1.38 billion ±$50 million revenue** and **$0.47 ±$0.04 adjusted EPS**, with about **$780 million adjusted operating expenses**. Data-center royalties more than doubled and Neoverse shipments exceeded 1.5 billion cores, but handset weakness and the timing of licensing contracts remain meaningful. [Full July call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/arm/transcripts/632326-q1-2027/); [issuer summary](https://newsroom.arm.com/news/arm-q1-fye27-results).

**Evidence gap:** Independent CPU shipment commitments, customer-level realized economics and synchronized industry revisions were not verified. A $2 billion demand pipeline is not $2 billion recognized revenue, and the 97% IP gross margin cannot be applied to all future CPU sales. The forecast separates those economic streams.

### Own-CPU working capital and funding

The move into chips requires deposits, inventory and factory partners even if Arm owns no leading-edge fab. A base feasibility path for fiscal 2027/2028/2029 uses **$6.1/$8.0/$10.0 billion revenue**, **15%/20%/25% common net margins**, **$250/$350/$450 million depreciation**, **$500/$700/$500 million incremental working capital** and **$600/$650/$700 million capex**. The resulting annual cash after investment is approximately **$65 million/$600 million/$1.750 billion**. The working-capital budgets explicitly include CPU inventory and manufacturing deposits; they are not funded by treating capacity reservations as customer revenue. Future compensation remains a cash-equivalent cost, with no SBC addback or duplicate future-grant dilution.

Against theJune **$3.888 billion cash/securities**, the path needs no assumed new common raise, before acquisitions or capital returns. An additional **$1.5 billion upfront manufacturing deposit** would consume much of the buffer but is arithmetically financeable if cash is accessible and other spending follows the forecast. A combined deposit shock, slower license collections and lower chip margins could require a new funding plan. These full fiscal-year proxies include months already elapsed sinceJune and do not claim exactOctober cash. **Evidence gap:** Definitive customer delivery contracts, manufacturing deposits and the final split between licensing and own-CPU costs were not verified; the margin and cash path remain conditional, which limits the score despite apparent liquidity.
