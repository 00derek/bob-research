## Investment thesis

Arm licenses processor designs and collects royalties when customers ship chips using its architecture. New designs and compute subsystems can raise royalty content per chip, while servers and AI devices offer growth beyond mature smartphones. The challenge is translating that opportunity into recurring GAAP earnings after engineering costs and dilution. The October 31 assessment remains AVOID: price exceeds the rebuilt bull present value, and the like-for-like reverse test requires earnings above the bull case. Normalized earnings and the evolving silicon strategy limit model confidence. Those uncertainties do not by themselves establish a credibility violation. The strongest opposing case is that richer v9 and CSS royalties, server adoption and purpose-built silicon create a much larger profit pool. The November result must show whether that revenue opportunity survives the associated cost.

## How the business creates value

Arm's licensing ecosystem spreads design costs across many chip vendors and benefits from a large developer base. Royalties depend on shipped units, architecture mix and contractual rates rather than announced design activity alone. Compute subsystems can sell more integrated intellectual property, but purpose-built silicon broadens competition with some customers and increases development expense. Smartphones provide scale; server and edge penetration provide upside but need adoption and software compatibility. The FY2029 base assumes $10 billion annual revenue and 25% net margin. That requires growth from the latest quarterly run rate with substantial margin expansion, not merely a larger total market.

### Five macro channels

- **Rates 1/5:** High required returns challenge a valuation concentrated in later earnings.
- **Inflation 1/5:** Design salaries and chip-development costs can outgrow royalty revenue.
- **Liquidity 2/5:** Customer and supplier funding conditions affect silicon programs and valuations.
- **Cycle 3/5:** Handset replacement and data-center build cycles influence licensed-chip shipments.
- **Policy 1/5:** Export restrictions and licensing disputes can alter the royalty opportunity.
