## Investment thesis

Credo sells high-speed connectivity products that improve reliability and power efficiency as AI computing systems grow. Active electrical cables, signal processing and optical products can increase its content in expanding networks, while vertical integration can broaden the available profit pool. The latest fiscal quarter confirms strong demand, but the old valuation treated very high adjusted margins too casually as ordinary net earnings. This review rebuilds the model with explicit GAAP-style net margins, higher dilution and a correct terminal date. The October 28 assessment remains EXIT: the old $160 closing line failed, and a price rebound does not create a new setup. The rebuilt weighted value is also below the quote. The strongest opposing case is that optical adoption and scale produce operating leverage closer to the earlier adjusted-profit assumptions.

**Research date October 28, 2026.** Completed-session quote **$173.69**; independent comparison **$173.84**, difference 0.086%. After-hours prices are excluded from closing triggers. Model date October 28; terminal date 2028-04-29.

## How the business creates value

Customers value reliable links because intermittent failures can reduce the useful output of expensive AI clusters. Credo's cables, retimers, SerDes and optical products address distances from within systems to between racks. Vertical integration through DustPhotonics can reduce external optical dependencies, but adds acquisition spending, intangible assets and engineering execution. New 1.6T products demonstrate a roadmap rather than recognized production revenue. The FY2028 base assumes $3.2 billion sales, about 67% above the latest quarter annualized, with a 32% net margin. That margin is above current GAAP profitability but below the roughly 49% non-GAAP margin, making the path demanding without simply using adjusted EPS as cash earnings.

### Five macro channels

- **Rates 1/5:** High required returns challenge premium multiples on a still-expanding profit base.
- **Inflation 1/5:** Foundry, packaging and engineering costs can dilute incremental margin.
- **Liquidity 4/5:** Customer financing and acquisition funding affect the timing and economics of growth.
- **Cycle 2/5:** AI deployment drives demand, while large customers can change capex cadence.
- **Policy 1/5:** Export rules and cross-border technology access can affect customers and suppliers.

The resulting macro assessment is **9/25**, an uptrend under pressure. V5.2 applies a 50% cap on normal exposure and starter/add pacing in this band; it does not mechanically reduce intrinsic value or create a stock-selection veto. The October 28 Fed decision kept the policy range at 3.75–4.00%; the official ten-year Treasury par yield was 5.05%. High rates remain material even after the rebound in equity indices.

**Evidence gap:** A complete product/customer concentration schedule, competitor order comparison and synchronized industry-wide estimate-revision history were not established.
