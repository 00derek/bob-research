## Investment thesis

Credo sells active electrical cables, signal processing and optical products that help costly computing systems communicate reliably and use less power. The **November 7, 2026 security assessment is EXIT**, using the **November 6 completed close $120.51**. A newly underwritten operating/capital model gives **$143.04 weighted present value**, **+18.7% price return**. Copper/optical growth must convert after compensation, working capital and acquisition obligations. The strongest opposing case is that optical products, AECs and customer diversification sustain growth far beyond current guidance with attractive operating leverage. ## Business economics and macro transmission

AECs address shorter connections where copper can offer attractive reliability and power economics; optical DSPs, silicon-photonics components and ZeroFlap transceivers address different distances and architectures. Moving toward a complete system can capture more value but adds qualification, inventory and customer-support exposure. Copper and optics can coexist rather than one permanently eliminating the other. Future near-packaged/co-packaged optics and memory-connectivity products create opportunity without already being booked revenue.

First-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue was **$479.00 million versus $223.07 million**, up approximately **115%**, and 9.6% sequentially. GAAP gross profit was **$309.08 million**, a **64.5% margin**, versus 67.4% a year earlier. Operating expenses rose from **$89.63 million to $188.38 million**; operating profit rose from **$60.74 million to $120.70 million**. Net income was **$129.43 million versus $63.40 million**, GAAP diluted EPS **$0.67 versus $0.34**. The quarter includes a small tax benefit, so the full reported net margin is not a normalized cash-tax benchmark.

The September call guides Q2 revenue **$525–535 million**, GAAP gross margin **62.9–64.9%** and GAAP operating expense **$199–204 million**, versus adjusted expense **$100–105 million**. Approximately half the difference is not a free operating saving; share compensation and acquisition-related costs explain much of it. Management expects **more than 85% full-year revenue growth**, **more than $600 million optical revenue**, and adjusted net margin around 50%. Those adjusted-profit expectations are not the model's common net margin after cash-equivalent compensation and ordinary taxes. [Primary release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026059795/credoq12027ex-991.htm); [full call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/crdo/transcripts/683506-q1-2027/).

The call's Q&A identifies four end customers at approximately **33%/28%/13%/10%**, **84% combined**. The filing's direct-customer table instead reports **43% and 28%** for its two largest customers. Those views use different customer definitions and should not be mixed. Greater product breadth across buyers can improve resilience, but a large end-customer budget change can still dominate sales. FY2028 NPO design wins and initial silicon-photonics revenue are distinct from established large recurring sales. Management's forecasts of transceiver market growth and future memory bandwidth remain expectations.

Astera's October 8 primary Aries 7 announcement demonstrates a competing connectivity roadmap at PCIe 7 speeds, while its June-quarter results show strong growth in existing products and planned switch ramps. These are adjacent competitive technologies, not perfectly substitutable products. Credo's reliability differentiation must earn repeat qualification and pricing as competitors invest. **Evidence gap:** Independent market share, comparable error rates, actual customer switching costs and synchronized industry revisions were not verified. [Astera primary product disclosure](https://ir.asteralabs.com/node/9336/pdf).
