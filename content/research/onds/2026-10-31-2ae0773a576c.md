## Investment thesis

Ondas combines autonomous systems, drones, counter-drone and related defense technologies. The opportunity is to turn specialized products and acquisitions into a larger platform serving government and critical-infrastructure customers. Orders and backlog show demand, but common equity benefits only after delivery costs, integration spending, working capital and new common claims. The August Q2 results show a fast-growing company with widening operating losses, rather than an established profitable platform. At $6.32 on October 30, the explicitly illustrative revenue-multiple model gives about $4.83 weighted value. The assessment is **Avoid**: fundamentals 9/30 fail the hard quality floor and the diluted-capital interpretation remains pending. The strongest opposing case is that larger backlog rapidly produces operating leverage and cash. That needs reported evidence, not an assumption that every acquired revenue dollar creates equivalent per-unit value.

## How the business creates value

The [October 30 issuer update](https://ir.ondas.com/) positions its counter-drone product range for a FEMA program. Eligibility and marketing do not establish awarded revenue, collections or profitable delivery, so this announcement does not repair the quality or diluted-capital gates.

Government, defense and infrastructure customers buy autonomous surveillance, protection and mission systems. Acquisition breadth can improve bids and cross-selling, but integration and procurement timing can make orders arrive well before collected cash. Reliability, certification and customer qualification can differentiate products; rapid expansion across businesses also consumes management attention and capital.

The [August 13 Q2 issuer release](https://ir.ondas.com/press-releases/detail/326/ondas-posts-record-q2-2026-revenue-of-83-8-million-on) reports $83.8 million sales, versus $50.1 million in Q1 and $6.3 million a year earlier. Reported backlog was $613 million, while $757 million pro-forma backlog includes acquisitions completed later. The FY2026 sales target is $525–550 million; it is not an organic growth rate. The [financial presentation](https://ir.ondas.com/all-sec-filings/content/0001193125-26-347973/onds-ex99_2.htm) shows $162.9 million operating loss and negative $50.6 million adjusted EBITDA. Reported net loss benefits from non-operating/fair-value items; they are not recurring operating profit.

Historical $1.4 billion cash/restricted cash/investments offers resources, but acquisitions, warrants, compensation and dilution determine the residual. **Evidence gap:** The current fully diluted common-claim, warrant, acquisition consideration and freely available cash bridge is incomplete. Delivery economics and integration returns also remain unproven. These are capital and quality restrictions, not a claim that defense demand is absent.

## Macro transmission

Rates affect acquisition funding and the discount on distant profits. Inflation changes component, labor and delivery costs; procurement prices may not adjust at the same speed. Risk liquidity can finance expansion but cannot demonstrate returns. Defense demand can be less consumer-cyclical, while procurement and project acceptance remain uneven. Export licenses, government budgets and national-security rules can create both opportunity and restrictions.

September PCE released October 29 showed 3.6% headline and 3.1% core annual inflation, while third-quarter real GDP grew at a 3.4% annual rate. Persistent inflation and growth leave long-duration earnings exposed to discount-rate pressure. The 50% normal-size cap and starter/add-only limit remain; macro modifies size rather than company conviction. The failure case is acquired growth that conceals persistent operating losses, higher contingent consideration, dilution or delayed customer collections. Watch organic versus acquired growth, adjusted and GAAP operating losses, cash use and warrants/common units. The 40% adverse-gap assumption is a stress judgment, not a maximum loss.
