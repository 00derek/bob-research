## Investment thesis

NVIDIA's products are turning AI infrastructure demand into exceptional revenue and operating profit. The investment case now also depends on customer financing and on the capital NVIDIA commits to help that demand become deployable. The rebuilt assessment is **WATCH** at the October 30 close of **$204.64**, against **$256.79** weighted present value, or 25.5% upside. A new prospective entry map is supported by current structure and the fresh operating model, but it cannot retroactively qualify yesterday's close. The strongest opposing case is that CUDA, networking and complete systems sustain much faster cash growth, producing $618.53 bull value. The $52.20 bear case instead combines weaker growth, lower margins and significant customer-support losses.

## How the business creates value

NVIDIA sells accelerated computing platforms combining GPUs, CPUs, networking, systems and software. CUDA and related tools reduce development friction, while an integrated rack can improve performance and deployment economics. A broad installed base and model compatibility support reuse. The economic test is whether customers earn adequate returns and can finance successive systems, and whether alternatives erode NVIDIA's share or pricing. Power, memory, packaging and deployment capacity can constrain delivery even when interest is strong.

### Commitments connect growth to financing risk

Note 10's primary table totals 366 billion future commitments, including 279 billion supply/capacity,29 billion cloud services,25 billion future data-center leases,25 billion equity investments and 8 billion capex. Supply alone increased from 119 billion the preceding quarter, with 92/87/88 billion scheduled in the fiscal 2027 remainder/2028/2029. Normal purchases become inventory and cost of sales; subtracting all 279 billion again from a model already paying for products would double count. Cancellation, rescheduling or reduced demand can still create incremental losses, which are stressed separately.

The additional 36 billion AI-cloud agreements are another economic exposure. Capacity can be sold by partners to third parties, reducing NVIDIA's obligation; the arrangements also support its research use. The model does not blindly add 29+36 as entirely distinct idle-capacity costs. Normal cloud/R&D expense remains within operating margins; incremental unused-capacity stresses are separate. Third-party data-center leases and reassignment assumptions require scrutiny as facilities commence.

The subsequent August SB Energy guarantees are particularly material: **up to 105 billion**, backing defined portions of OpenAI-related lease/power obligations across 4.25 GW and nine phases. They generally take effect as construction phases enter service, beginning fiscal 2029, decline with payments and can terminate on specified conditions including a satisfactory customer credit rating. A further 3.8 GW is discretionary optional support, not an existing binding 105-billion-plus obligation. The support can secure NVIDIA-only infrastructure and future sales while exposing common equity if customer economics fail.

These commitments are not all current debt, and they are not costless demand confirmation. The model stresses a 105 billion gross guarantee call in 2030 bear,20 billion base and 5 billion bull, with separate idle/stranded-capacity charges. Timing and amounts are analyst loss stresses, not the issuer's expected liability. A full gross call ignores potential recovery and is deliberately severe; actual phases and defaults could differ substantially.

## What could break the thesis

Demand can be economically real yet unfinanceable at the pace modeled. Customer concentration, export restrictions, a rack transition, power delays or custom silicon may reduce returns. One direct customer represented 16% of revenue, so booking growth does not ensure diversified collections. Inventory and supply commitments magnify a pause; equity investments and guarantees can fail at the same time as product margins. Credibility is **PASS** on reviewed evidence; support arrangements alone do not establish improper revenue recognition. Binary risk is **HIGH**, with 20% plausible-gap stress, not an assured maximum loss.
