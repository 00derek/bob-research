## Investment thesis

Bloom Energy sells on-site fuel-cell systems that can supply reliable power when customers face long waits for grid connections. AI data centers and other electricity-intensive businesses create a credible demand mechanism, and the October Q3 report shows rapid sales and profit growth. The investment still depends on cash conversion and the capital needed to manufacture and install that equipment. The October 31 assessment is TRIM: the rebuilt weighted value is well below the completed-session price despite an ambitious long-term growth forecast. The strongest opposing case is that power shortages and execution keep growth and margins near the bull path for several years. The largest warning is that positive accounting profit has not yet become self-funding quarterly cash. This review replaces the previously blocked model with an explicit post-results capital and operating bridge, while retaining its uncertainty.

## How the business creates value

Customers buy electricity availability and deployment speed, not just fuel-cell equipment. Bloom's opportunity increases when utility interconnection delays make on-site generation valuable, but equipment must be delivered, installed and maintained at acceptable cost. Product revenue, service obligations and financing arrangements have different cash profiles; a large order or customer deposit does not equal free cash earned. The FY2030 base assumes $14 billion revenue, about 3.1 times the midpoint of the new 2026 guidance, requiring roughly 33% compound annual growth over four fiscal years. A 16% sustainable net margin is below Q3's reported margin, allowing some cost normalization, but it still assumes profitable factory expansion and customer collections.

### Five macro channels

- **Rates 1/5:** Debt, customer project finance and distant terminal earnings all face a high cost of capital.
- **Inflation 1/5:** Equipment, fuel and installation costs affect both supplier margins and customer payback.
- **Liquidity 2/5:** Financing access matters while growth absorbs working capital and capex.
- **Cycle 3/5:** AI demand supports power spending, but project schedules and customer budgets can still slip.
- **Policy 1/5:** Permits, emissions rules and energy policy directly affect deployment and returns.

## What could break the thesis

Installation delays, warranty performance, natural-gas economics, emissions and permitting can also change customer returns. Watch quarterly operating cash, customer deposits, contract-asset conversion, capex, debt obligations and diluted units.
