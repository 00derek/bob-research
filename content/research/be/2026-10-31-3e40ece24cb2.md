## Investment thesis

Bloom Energy converts natural gas or other fuels into electricity using solid-oxide fuel cells. Data-center customers value dependable power that can be installed before a grid connection is available. That supports the business opportunity, but customer deposits, project acceptance and factory investment determine how much accounting growth becomes distributable cash. The October 31 assessment is **TRIM, conditionally 50%, at $203.17**, the October 30 close. A newly built operating cash-flow forecast gives **$110.79 weighted present value**. The quote exceeds 115% of weighted value; the proposed 50% trim is the framework's security-level response to that valuation band, conditional on an applicable exposure and ordinary execution checks. It is not evidence that any transaction occurred. The strongest opposing case is that power scarcity allows Bloom to sustain exceptionally rapid growth and high returns on incremental factory capital. The bull case explicitly models that outcome. [September 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1664703/000162828026066931/be-20260930.htm).

## Business economics and growth

Bloom sells equipment and services, with customer economics depending on gas delivery, electricity prices, reliability, emissions rules, maintenance and financing. Fuel cells can solve a timing problem for customers, but do not eliminate fuel expense or the cost of service obligations. Oracle's announced expansion contemplates **up to 2.8 GW**; the maximum is not the same as firm orders recognized in revenue. Joint-venture arrangements and customer financing also differ from unrestricted parent cash. [April Oracle announcement](https://investor.bloomenergy.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Bloom-Energy-and-Oracle-Expand-Strategic-Partnership-to-Deploy-up-to-2-8-GW-to-Accelerate-AI-Infrastructure-Build-Out/default.aspx).

Third-quarter revenue increased from **$519.05 million to $1.236 billion**, including **$1.074 billion product revenue**. Gross margin was approximately **33.8%** and operating profit **$225.45 million**, versus **$7.85 million**. Common net income was **$250.27 million versus a $23.09 million loss**; diluted EPS **$0.78**. A tax charge of only **$0.82 million** is not an ordinary long-run tax rate. Diluted weighted shares increased to **324.29 million from 234.93 million**, so operating growth cannot be translated into per-share growth without the equity bridge.

Management raised full-year guidance to **$4.4–4.6 billion revenue, $960–1,030 million adjusted operating profit and $3.10–3.40 adjusted EPS**. These are adjusted measures for 2026, not a 2027 promise. The call emphasizes modular capacity expansion and management's attractive expected factory payback. Those are operating plans whose economics must be confirmed by cash collections, yields, deployment and service costs. [Full October 22 call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/be/transcripts/717313-q3-2026/).

Caterpillar's October 29 release independently reports **18% Power & Energy sales growth to $8.784 billion**, with data centers contributing to increased turbine and large reciprocating-engine sales. This corroborates power demand and demonstrates capable competing supply; it does not establish identical cost, delivery or emissions performance. **Evidence gap:** Comparable delivered cost per kWh, installed service profitability and synchronized industry estimate revisions were not verified. The forecast credits demand without assuming Bloom owns the entire power-shortage market. [Caterpillar primary results](https://investors.caterpillar.com/news/news-details/2026/Caterpillar-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx).
