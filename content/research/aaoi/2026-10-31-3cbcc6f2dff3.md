## Investment thesis

Applied Optoelectronics makes optical transceivers and cable-network equipment. Its vertically integrated manufacturing can capture demand for faster AI links, but scaling that manufacturing requires substantial cash before it produces reliable shareholder profits. The October 31 signal is **EXIT**: the **October 30 close of $69.05** remains below the previously declared **$89 closing risk reference**, and a rebuilt model gives only **$71.79 weighted value**, about **4.0% upside**. That modest expected return cannot qualify a replacement setup or erase the prior closing-risk failure. The strongest opposing case is that 800 G/1.6 T demand and new factories create operating leverage; the bull tests it with much higher revenue and margins. The uncertainty is how much cash and dilution it takes to reach that outcome. [June 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1158114/000143774926026278/aaoi20260630_10q.htm).

## Business economics and growth dependencies

Cloud customers buy transceivers that turn electrical data into light, while cable operators buy HFC equipment for broadband upgrades. Qualification and integration provide some customer stickiness, and domestic production can diversify supply. Optical performance, cost, yields and delivery matter more than the fact that a product has a faster headline speed. Coherent, Lumentum and Asian module producers compete for the same budgets and components; demand growth alone does not establish pricing power.

The latest released quarter is **June 30**, reported **August 6**; November 5 results remain future. Revenue grew from **$102.952 million to $191.922 million**, while gross profit rose from **$31.162 million to $53.207 million**. GAAP gross margin fell from approximately **30.3% to 27.7%**, operating loss widened from **$15.976 million to $24.727 million**, and net loss from **$9.098 million to $22.781 million**. Adjusted profitability should therefore not be substituted for GAAP economics. Quarterly loss-period weighted shares of **81.568 million** exclude claims that would dilute a profitable company.

The call reports **$107.7 million data-center revenue**, including **$48.4 million 400 G** and **$12.8 million 800 G**. Management expected nearly fivefold sequential 800 G growth in Q3, but also identified weak 100 G shipments from a customer's memory-related switch shortage. Q3 sales guidance is **$255–290 million**, adjusted gross margin **29–30.5%**, and adjusted EPS **$0.11–0.26** on **92.8 million diluted shares**. The approximately **$1.1 billion 2026 sales aspiration** needs a much stronger second half. Production capacity and component availability remain constraints; promised demand cannot recognize itself as revenue. [Full August call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/aaoi/transcripts/662748-q2-2026/); [issuer results](https://investors.ao-inc.com/news-releases/news-release-details/applied-optoelectronics-reports-second-quarter-2026-results).

**Evidence gap:** Independent competitor yields, customer share and a synchronized industry revision panel were not verified. The issuer's capacity and demand claims are differentiated from independent proof. Top-ten customers represented approximately 99% of first-half revenue, and purchase orders generally do not provide commitments beyond a year, increasing forecast sensitivity to a few buyers.

Net margins include ordinary taxes and financing.

Starting from the earlier base's roughly **$99 million end2026 cash**, an illustrative **10 million new equity units issued at $50 for $500 million** leaves approximately **$365 million after 2027 and $525 million after 2028**, before other commitments. This explicitly uses part of the 115 million terminal claim budget: the roughly 101.4 million gross existing/common-convertible-Amazon claim floor plus 10 million financing units is about 111.4 million, leaving around 3.6 million uncertainty capacity. That remaining capacity is not a promise of free shares or cash. The equity raise is hypothetical and conditional on buyers; its proceeds are not separately added as a premium to the P/E valuation.

The budget assumes much of the 2026 factory build and equipment prepayments installs usable capacity, allowing capex to fall despite rising sales. That is demanding. At **$600 million capex in each of 2027 and 2028**, the additional $600 million spending consumes the $525 million cushion and requires about $75 million more finance, approximately 1.5 million units at $50. Continuing $950 million annual capex would require far more capital and invalidate the 115 million base claim budget unless operating cash or financing terms improve. **Evidence gap:** Factory completion costs, working-capital intensity and future equity pricing remain uncertain.
