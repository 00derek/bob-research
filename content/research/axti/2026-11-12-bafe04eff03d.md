## Business economics and macro transmission

Customers buy wafers with difficult purity and uniformity requirements, qualify them for device production and value reliable delivery. Three-inch to four-inch migration can increase usable output and selling prices, while successful six-inch material could extend the opportunity. Qualification, yield and export approvals can delay revenue despite strong customer demand. Management reported **$54.3 million indium-phosphide revenue**, more than **$500 million backlog**, allocations across customers and an intention to double capacity during 2027. Its approximately **$90 million fourth-quarter opportunity** is described as supported by permits, not a guarantee of shipment acceptance or cash collection. Backlog combines longer contracts, shorter orders and spot demand; it is not all next-quarter revenue. [October call](https://earningscalls.dev/transcripts/axt-inc_axti_earnings_call_transcript_2026-10-29).

This is a scarcity business whose economics can attract new capacity. AI optical links provide structural demand, but the share of substrate spending captured by AXT depends on wafer size, customer yields and competing suppliers. Sumitomo and other qualified producers make it inappropriate to model a permanent monopoly. **Evidence gap:** Targeted competitor searches did not establish an independently verified, current supplier-share or six-inch yield table. The few-producer and allocation claims remain management evidence, not independent confirmation. The model consequently permits strong growth but compresses margins in the bear case.

[Full material 8-K description](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000121390026113058/ea0309774-8k_axt.htm).

The funding bridge can be investigated rather than assumed impossible. June liquid resources were **$748.8 million**, with only **$75.1 million in foreign-bank cash and investments**, including **$32.9 million restricted**. Subtracting the foreign-bank total implies roughly **$673.7 million outside foreign banks**, a geographic proxy rather than a legal parent-only balance sheet. April's parent offering generated **$600.1 million net proceeds** explicitly intended in part for Tongmei expansion. These facts support a funded $400 million subscription without assuming a mandatory new common offering. The illustrative outside-foreign-bank remainder is **$273.7 million**, before intervening cash movements and transfer costs. Consolidated unrestricted September liquidity of **$784.694 million** would become approximately **$1.070–1.330 billion** after outside proceeds, before expansion spending; the internal transfer does not change that total. The scenarios still allow 75–80 million diluted parent shares against 65.862 million quarterly shares, rather than promising no dilution.

Investors receive priority distributions and redemption rights if specified conditions occur, including failure to obtain a qualified IPO within three years. Redemption includes **8% annual compounded return plus unpaid distributions**. On $545 million, three years would produce approximately **$686.54 million before distributions**. Tongmei is the initial obligor; AXT **may elect, but is not obligated**, to purchase instead. A qualified IPO must meet the agreement's **13% IRR** condition. These are distinct conditions, not interchangeable discount rates. Notes or share settlement can introduce additional financing or dilution; 90-day VWAP provisions matter if shares are used. Closing deadlines and approval extensions can change the path.
