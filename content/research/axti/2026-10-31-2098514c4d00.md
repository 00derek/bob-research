## Investment thesis

AXT supplies compound-semiconductor substrates used in optical links, wireless and industrial devices. Indium phosphide demand is translating into revenue and unusually high margins: September-quarter sales rose to **$73.410 million**, from **$27.955 million** a year earlier. The security assessment is **AVOID at $55.06**, rather than treating that acceleration as sufficient evidence of value. A newly rebuilt October 31 model gives about **$43.6 weighted present value**. Capacity expansion, export permission and new subsidiary investors determine how much growth eventually belongs to each AXT share. The strongest opposing case is sustained scarcity, successful larger wafers and a billion-dollar annual revenue business; the bull case makes those assumptions explicit. [October 29 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926033941/ex_1013304.htm).

## Business, demand and competitive position

Customers buy wafers with difficult purity and uniformity requirements, qualify them for device production and value reliable delivery. Three-inch to four-inch migration can increase usable output and selling prices, while successful six-inch material could extend the opportunity. Qualification, yield and export approvals can delay revenue despite strong customer demand. Management reported **$54.3 million indium-phosphide revenue**, more than **$500 million backlog**, allocations across customers and an intention to double capacity during 2027. Its approximately **$90 million fourth-quarter opportunity** is described as supported by permits, not a guarantee of shipment acceptance or cash collection. Backlog combines longer contracts, shorter orders and spot demand; it is not all next-quarter revenue. [October call](https://earningscalls.dev/transcripts/axt-inc_axti_earnings_call_transcript_2026-10-29).

This is a scarcity business whose economics can attract new capacity. AI optical links provide structural demand, but the share of substrate spending captured by AXT depends on wafer size, customer yields and competing suppliers. Sumitomo and other qualified producers make it inappropriate to model a permanent monopoly. **Evidence gap:** Targeted competitor searches did not establish an independently verified, current supplier-share or six-inch yield table. The few-producer and allocation claims remain management evidence, not independent confirmation. The model consequently permits strong growth but compresses margins in the bear case.

## Financial health and the financing claims

Quarterly gross profit was **$41.095 million**, operating income **$28.427 million** and net income attributable to AXT **$25.723 million**, compared with a **$1.906 million attributable loss** a year earlier. Gross margin reached approximately **56.0%**, up from **44.9% in June**. Net interest income of **$5.895 million** contributed materially; the future earnings model reduces this contribution as expansion uses cash. Diluted shares increased from **43.842 million to 65.862 million**, so company earnings growth is not the same as per-share growth. [Quarterly statements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926033941/ex_1013304.htm).

September cash, restricted cash and investments total **$825.894 million**, including **$41.2 million restricted cash**. That headline is not unrestricted money attributable solely to parent common equity. Short-term loans were **$72.461 million**, other long-term liabilities **$42.930 million**, redeemable minority interests **$42.287 million**, and parent preferred carrying value **$3.532 million**. Other long-term liabilities are not assumed to be entirely financial debt. Parent Series A has 883,000 nonconvertible shares, a 5% cumulative dividend and $4 per-share liquidation preference; June liquidation preference including arrears was **$8.317 million**. The forecast deducts the approximately **$176,600 annual preferred dividend** from parent earnings.

The latest filed cash-flow statement is June's: first-half operating cash was **negative $0.900 million**, against **$8.598 million cash capex**, for **negative $9.498 million CFO less cash capex**. Another **$6.401 million fixed assets financed with notes** shows why cash-only capex understates resource commitments. The September call's **$2.6 million is depreciation and amortization, not capex**. The **$43.5 million Lumentum prepayment** finances fulfillment and is a customer obligation until delivery; it must not be counted again as new revenue or free cash. **Evidence gap:** A search of SEC submissions found no September 10-Q yet. September CFO, capex, precise legal-entity cash location and final customer-liability reconciliation therefore remain unavailable after retrieval attempts. [Latest filed 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926027677/axti20260630_10q.htm).

### What the Tongmei recapitalization changes

The October 27 agreement requires AXT to subscribe **$400 million** to Tongmei before closing; outside investors may contribute **$285–545 million**. At maximum outside funding AXT ownership falls from **85.5% to approximately 73.4%**. The parent transfer is within the consolidated group: subtracting $400 million from consolidated cash as though it vanished would be wrong. Outside cash increases subsidiary liquidity but comes with minority priority and redemption rights, so adding it dollar for dollar to parent common fair value would also be wrong. [Full material 8-K description](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000121390026113058/ea0309774-8k_axt.htm).

The funding bridge can be investigated rather than assumed impossible. June liquid resources were **$748.8 million**, with only **$75.1 million in foreign-bank cash and investments**, including **$32.9 million restricted**. Subtracting the foreign-bank total implies roughly **$673.7 million outside foreign banks**, a geographic proxy rather than a legal parent-only balance sheet. April's parent offering generated **$600.1 million net proceeds** explicitly intended in part for Tongmei expansion. These facts support a funded $400 million subscription without assuming a mandatory new common offering. The illustrative outside-foreign-bank remainder is **$273.7 million**, before intervening cash movements and transfer costs. Consolidated unrestricted September liquidity of **$784.694 million** would become approximately **$1.070–1.330 billion** after outside proceeds, before expansion spending; the internal transfer does not change that total. The scenarios still allow 72–78 million diluted parent shares against 65.862 million quarterly shares, rather than promising no dilution.

Investors receive priority distributions and redemption rights if specified conditions occur, including failure to obtain a qualified IPO within three years. Redemption includes **8% annual compounded return plus unpaid distributions**. On $545 million, three years would produce approximately **$686.55 million before distributions**. Tongmei is the initial obligor; AXT **may elect, but is not obligated**, to purchase instead. A qualified IPO must meet the agreement's **13% IRR** condition. These are distinct conditions, not interchangeable discount rates. Notes or share settlement can introduce additional financing or dilution; 90-day VWAP provisions matter if shares are used. Closing deadlines and approval extensions can change the path.

**Evidence gap:** The 8-K says the complete agreements will be filed later. Their full priority waterfall and September legal-entity funding bridge cannot yet be audited. This is a specific residual limitation, not a reason to retain an unexamined generic model blocker. No surplus cash premium is awarded, and the bear case recognizes lower margins, financing pressure and a reduced multiple.
