## Investment thesis

AXT makes compound-semiconductor substrates used in optical devices and other specialized chips. Indium phosphide demand can rise as AI networks need faster optical connections, and additional manufacturing capacity can turn that demand into higher sales. The latest quarter shows a meaningful recovery from the prior loss period, with better gross margins and positive net income. The October 28 assessment remains AVOID because the price exceeds even the rebuilt bull present value, while funding and minority economic claims limit model confidence. The stronger balance-sheet evidence improves business quality above the framework minimum; it does not cure the reverse-valuation block. The strongest opposing case is that scarce substrate capacity and fast AI demand allow revenue and margins to exceed the bull case. That requires evidence beyond the current quarterly run rate and must survive further dilution.

## How the business creates value

Optical-device makers use indium phosphide wafers because ordinary silicon cannot provide the same optoelectronic performance. Qualification, crystal quality and reliable output matter to customers, while AXT's integrated materials exposure can support supply. The same integration adds capital needs, geographic and minority-interest complexity. Higher substrate demand is useful only if saleable yield, capacity and customer acceptance improve together. The FY2028 base assumes $400 million revenue and a 12% net margin versus Q2 sales of $47.6 million; it therefore needs more than twice the annualized quarterly revenue. Competitors, alternate optical architectures and export restrictions can limit the duration of shortages and pricing power.

### Five macro channels

- **Rates 1/5:** High required returns penalize earnings that depend on a later capacity ramp.
- **Inflation 1/5:** Energy and materials costs can offset wafer price improvements.
- **Liquidity 4/5:** Access to financing matters before new production capacity earns cash.
- **Cycle 2/5:** Optical customer capex drives orders; a secular demand theme still has inventory cycles.
- **Policy 1/5:** Export licensing and concentrated manufacturing geography can directly interrupt sales.

The resulting macro assessment is **9/25**, an uptrend under pressure. V5.2 applies a 50% cap on normal exposure and starter/add pacing in this band; it does not mechanically reduce intrinsic value or create a stock-selection veto. The October 28 Fed decision kept the policy range at 3.75–4.00%; the official ten-year Treasury par yield was 5.05%. High rates remain material even after the rebound in equity indices.

**Evidence gap:** A complete product/customer concentration schedule, competitor order comparison and synchronized industry-wide estimate-revision history were not established. These omissions limit confidence in the forecast bridge and industry score; the next filing and peer results should test them.
