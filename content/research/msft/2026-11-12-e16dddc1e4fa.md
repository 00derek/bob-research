## Investment thesis

Microsoft sells cloud computing, productivity software and enterprise infrastructure. Switching costs and a broad sales channel can monetize AI use, while short-lived equipment, finance leases and customer economics determine how much of accounting profit becomes owner cash. The **November 12 assessment is TRIM**. The new model gives **$309.67 weighted present value (-40.6%)**, against the **$521.13 November 11 completed close**. The conditional security-level reduction target is 50% because price exceeds 115% of weighted value.

The November 10 Anthropic prospectus identifies a conditional $1 billion Microsoft placement and amended $47.2–49.2 billion compute commitments through June 2033. The model reserves the equity cash once and charges an incremental interest stress for the $25 billion notes documented in the November 4 indenture. The strongest opposing case is that Azure consumption and software pricing monetize AI faster than short-lived equipment and lease costs grow, leaving substantially more owner cash. **Evidence gap:** Actual note coupon mix, customer compute draw timing, financing repayments, replacement needs and future lease commencements remain uncertain. The 6% coupon stress is an analyst assumption, not a claimed weighted actual coupon.

## Business economics and competitive position

### How the business creates value

Microsoft bundles productivity software, identity, security, development tools and cloud infrastructure into customer workflows. Switching costs and a large sales channel help it distribute AI services. Azure's consumption revenue can rise as customers move from training models to running applications, while subscriptions can capture some of that value through pricing. Competition comes from AWS and Google Cloud, specialized GPU clouds, open-source software and customers optimizing their own compute. Efficiency can lower Microsoft's costs and stimulate usage, but can also lower customer spending per task.
