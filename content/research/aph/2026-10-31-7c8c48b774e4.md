## Investment thesis

Amphenol supplies connectors, cables and sensors that carry signals and power through data centers, vehicles, industrial systems and defense equipment. Qualification, engineering and local customer relationships can make small components economically important. The deep October 31 assessment is **WATCH at $79.30**, the October 30 close. A new operating forecast gives **$93.83 weighted present value**, about **18.3% upside**, below the 25% required for a starter. The strongest opposing case is that AI connection complexity and the CommScope acquisition sustain unusually rapid growth. The model recognizes substantial growth but also the acquisition debt, working capital and current tax rate; it does not treat every percentage point of reported growth as organic. All share and price figures use the September **2-for-1 split** basis. [September 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/820313/000110465926120646/aph-20260930x10q.htm).

## Business economics and growth

Customers choose reliable components that fit demanding designs, sometimes with high qualification costs relative to the component's price. Higher data rates and power density increase engineering needs. TE Connectivity and specialist suppliers also compete for these budgets; the opportunity is broader than one exclusive AI connector. Diversification helps, but IT datacom has become a large enough business that its order cycle matters substantially.

Third-quarter sales increased from **$6.194 billion to $10.230 billion**: **65% reported growth versus 37% organic**, with acquisitions providing much of the difference. Gross profit was **$4.016 billion**, operating income **$3.021 billion** and net income attributable to Amphenol **$2.034 billion**, compared with **$1.246 billion** a year earlier. Operating margin was **29.5%**. Orders of approximately **$12.2 billion** produced **1.19 book-to-bill**, supporting near-term demand rather than proving a permanent growth rate. Q4 guidance is **$10.3–10.5 billion sales and $0.81–0.83 adjusted EPS**. [October 28 issuer release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/820313/000110465926119421/aph-ex99d1.htm).

The call reports IT datacom sales up **117% reported and 77% organically**, with AI approximately three-quarters of that market's sales. Communications networks, however, declined **28% organically**. Acquisition growth and different market cycles therefore need separate interpretation. CommScope broadens fiber/connectivity capability, while the latest smaller deals expand sensors and related applications; future deal authorization is not already acquired revenue. A new $4 billion repurchase program and 20% dividend increase describe capital intentions, not completed future purchases. [Full October call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/aph/transcripts/659916-q3-2026/).

TE's own results also describe increasing AI orders, corroborating industry demand while demonstrating competition. **Evidence gap:** Comparable current market shares, customer-level overlap and a synchronized estimate-revision panel were not verified. The model treats design wins and AI exposure as evidence of opportunity, without assigning an exclusive share of an undefined market. [TE issuer results](https://investors.te.com/financial-reports/quarterly-results/default.aspx).

### Cash and funding feasibility

The base forecast also has to fund its growth. At $54 billion sales, **30% operating margin gives $16.2 billion EBIT**; subtracting **$0.92 billion net financing**, applying **27% ordinary tax**, and deducting approximately **$84 million minority income** produces about **$11.07 billion common earnings**, matching the 20.5% net-margin model. This does not require a special tax benefit or free acquisition debt.

An illustrative 2028 cash budget adds **$2.2 billion depreciation/amortization**, takes no compensation addback, and deducts **$1.7 billion incremental working capital**, producing about **$11.57 billion CFO**. **$2.16 billion capex**, about 4% of sales, leaves **$9.41 billion CFO less capex**. Dividends **$1.8 billion**, repurchases **$1.5 billion** and debt repayment **$3 billion** leave approximately **$3.11 billion cash**. A 2027 test at $45 billion sales/20% margin, $1.9 billion depreciation, $1.3 billion working capital and $1.8 billion capex produces about **$7.8 billion free cash**, covering $1.5 billion dividends, $1.33 billion repurchases and $2.5 billion debt repayment, with approximately $2.47 billion remaining. Starting September liquidity less about $1.6 billion announced deals is roughly $3.7 billion before subsequent operating cash; unspecified new acquisitions are not assumed free.

This is an analyst feasibility budget, not a management cash guide. If 2028 working-capital investment is $4 billion instead of $1.7 billion, residual cash falls to about **$0.81 billion** before unplanned deals. Repurchases and deleveraging would need to adjust if margins, collection or integration disappoint. The base's interest allowance remains meaningful even as debt is scheduled lower; the calculation does not depend on refinancing at zero cost. **Evidence gap:** Acquisition timing, amortization, seasonal working capital and future borrowing rates could change this bridge materially.
