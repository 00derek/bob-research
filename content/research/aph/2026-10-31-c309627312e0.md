## Investment thesis

Amphenol supplies connectors, cable systems and sensors used across data centers, communications, industrial equipment and transportation. More complex electronic systems can increase its content per device, while a diversified manufacturing network and acquisitions extend customer reach. The October 28 quarter supports that business case: orders and organic growth remain strong, and cash conversion is substantial. ## How the business creates value

Electrical and optical interconnects are small components within expensive systems, but failures can be costly, creating value for qualified suppliers with reliable delivery. Amphenol serves many end markets, reducing dependence on a single product cycle while expanding AI-related data-center content. Local business units and acquisitions broaden product coverage; acquisitions also add goodwill, debt and integration work. Q3 order intake supports near-term visibility but is not guaranteed future sales. The FY2027 base forecast of $43 billion revenue is about 16% above the midpoint of 2026 management guidance, requiring ongoing organic growth and acquired contributions. The model assumes a 21% adjusted net margin after interest, not the almost 30% operating margin.

### Five macro channels

- **Rates 1/5:** Borrowing costs matter directly to the acquisition-funded debt base and equity valuation.
- **Inflation 1/5:** Material, labor and energy costs influence manufacturing margin and customer budgets.
- **Liquidity 2/5:** Financial market access affects acquisition financing; it does not change order economics.
- **Cycle 3/5:** Industrial and transport orders can offset or amplify AI-related demand.
- **Policy 1/5:** Global manufacturing, tariffs and defense procurement affect access and costs.

## What could break the thesis

A connector supplier can report strong orders while customers double-order around bottlenecks. Subsequent digestion would lower utilization and pressure the multiple. Acquisition integration and financing can also dilute returns despite higher reported revenue. Monitor organic sales against acquired contributions, order cancellations, free cash flow relative to adjusted profit, and net debt. New acquisitions financed with cash and debt require an earnings-and-interest bridge. The 15% gap stress is an analyst assumption for event planning, not a predicted loss.
