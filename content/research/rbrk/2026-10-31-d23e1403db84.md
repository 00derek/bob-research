## Investment thesis

Rubrik sells subscriptions that help organizations recover data and identities after cyberattacks, and is expanding into AI-agent security. Retention and new customer demand support a real growth business. The investment question is how much remains for common equity after compensation, hosting, sales costs and reinvestment. This October 31 rebuild gives **$36.27 weighted present value**, versus the **$124.15 October 30 close**, and a **$98.85 bull estimate**. The framework assessment is **EXIT** because the quote exceeds the modeled bull boundary while weighted expected return is negative. Reverse valuation also blocks a new entry. The strongest opposing case is faster adoption and better cash margins than even this optimistic forecast. Discount rates and terminal economics matter materially, so these estimates are conditional judgments. The earlier $121.72 earnings-multiple estimate is superseded by a newly constructed cash-flow model; this is a change in underwriting, not an assertion of a new earnings release. [Latest actual earnings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1943896/000194389626000055/rubrikinc-991pressrelease7.htm).

## How the business creates value

Customers need usable recovery copies, clean identity systems and restoration workflows after attackers evade prevention. Integrations, tested recovery procedures and growing protected datasets can encourage renewals and expansion. Rubrik must keep its products reliable and earn enough incremental spending to offset competition, cloud delivery costs and sales effort. ARR measures subscription run-rate; it is neither recognized revenue nor immediately distributable cash.

The latest actual quarter ended **July 31**, released **August 27**. Subscription ARR reached **$1.66 billion, up 33%**; revenue was **$427.26 million versus $309.86 million** yearly. GAAP gross margin was **78.4% versus 79.5%**, operating loss **$71.90 million versus $94.46 million**, and common loss **$61.78 million versus $95.93 million**. Adjusted EPS of **$0.20** differs from the **$0.30 GAAP loss**. Improving scale is visible, but recurring compensation cannot be removed merely because it is paid in equity. [Full quarterly statements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1943896/000194389626000060/rbrk-20260731.htm).

Management reports net retention above **119%**, **3,084 customers** exceeding $100,000 ARR and approximately **88% of ARR** from those larger customers. It describes early Flex bundled-contract adoption and more than 15 Agent Cloud customers. These observations support cross-selling, but early product customers do not establish mature recurring profits. On the full call, the CFO explicitly said Strata contributed **zero ARR** to the quarter and full-year outlook. The model therefore does not count an acquired ARR windfall. [Full August call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rbrk/transcripts/686066-q2-2027/).

Strata adds identity orchestration and continuity. The June 9 primary announcement does not disclose its individual purchase price and labels the new capabilities private preview; product availability and customer acceptance still matter. The quarter's aggregate June acquisition accounting is not allocated to each target in this review. [Primary acquisition and product announcement](https://www.rubrik.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/26/rubrik-advances-identity-resilience-through-strata-acquisition-and-identity-roll-forward-innovation).

Competition is active. Commvault's October 27 primary release reports **$1.104 billion subscription ARR, up 20%**, and **$269 million subscription revenue, up 21%**. These are different mixes and definitions, rather than a synchronized market-share ranking. They demonstrate a capable rival investing in the same demand. Veeam, Cohesity, cloud providers and bundled security offerings also contest recovery budgets. [Current Commvault results](https://www.commvault.com/news/commvault-announces-second-quarter-fiscal-2027-financial-results).

**Evidence gap:** Product-level recurring revenue, customer acquisition cost, comparable recovery outcomes and synchronized peer revisions are incomplete. The forecast requires sustained expansion and new customer wins; subsequent disclosures must show those mechanisms actually converting to cash.

### Customers, margins and capital needed

One base operating requirement is approximately **$6.5 billion exit ARR**, supporting $5.9 billion recognized annual revenue with timing differences. For illustration, **5,000 large customers averaging $1 million ARR** supply $5 billion, and smaller customers another $1.5 billion. The current larger-customer run-rate averages roughly $474,000; the model thus requires both more large customers and much higher spending per customer. These are forecast requirements, not signed contracts. If the starting ARR expands at 12% net annually for seven years it reaches approximately $3.67 billion; reaching $6.5 billion also needs about $2.8 billion from new customers and additional products. This does not extrapolate 119% retention forever.

Base terminal gross margin **80%**, less **20% R&D, 27% sales/marketing and 8% administration**, gives **25% EBIT**.
