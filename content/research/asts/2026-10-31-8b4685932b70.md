## Investment thesis

AST SpaceMobile is building a satellite network intended to connect ordinary mobile phones through cellular operators. The engineering achievement and the investment case are different questions: throughput tests do not establish paying utilization, recurring revenue or sufficient construction funding. The October 31 assessment is **AVOID at $40.97**, the October 30 close. A newly built cash-flow model gives **$13.25 weighted present value**, with a zero bear case, a roughly $2 base and a much higher bull. Even the base requires additional financing before positive cash generation. The strongest opposing case is rapid network deployment followed by high utilization, attractive operator economics and government support. The model gives that outcome a meaningful 20% probability, while charging for satellites, working capital and compensation. [June 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1780312/000119312526342550/asts-20260630.htm).

## Business economics and growth

AST supplies space-based capacity rather than a standalone retail cellular subscription. Mobile operators provide distribution, spectrum access and customer relationships; their economics and contractual revenue sharing matter as much as satellite capacity. Large antennas, spectrum coordination, launch reliability, replacement satellites, ground equipment and regulators all sit between a successful demonstration and sustainable common earnings. Service is not an exclusive AST market. T-Mobile already markets competing satellite connectivity; competing coverage and pricing can limit adoption and the fraction of subscriber spending available to AST. [T-Mobile service description](https://www.t-mobile.com/coverage/satellite-phone-service).

Second-quarter revenue was **$31.52 million versus $1.16 million**, comprising **$24.43 million products and $7.09 million services**. This is a real increase but is not $31.5 million of recurring consumer mobile usage. Related-party gateway product sales contributed approximately **$1.92 million**. The August call retained **$150–200 million fiscal 2026 revenue guidance** and described government-funded opportunities across 2026–27. Contract opportunities and potential Japanese support are not unrestricted cash already received. [Full August call](https://openfilings.org/companies/us/ASTS/transcripts/1a1e19cd-5084-4239-affc-c8e723037623).

The call described **13 spacecraft in orbit**, including test/demonstration hardware, rather than 13 fully commercial BlueBird satellites. A reported **98.9 Mbps peak test** demonstrates capability under a test configuration; it is not average customer throughput or utilization. Management discussed approximately **45 satellites by early 2027** and BlueBirds 14–16 preparing for shipment. Those are dated operational targets, not independently confirmed October launch completions. **Evidence gap:** The complete current launch-by-launch commercial constellation and paying subscriber utilization could not be verified from a current primary operating table. The forecast therefore assumes deployment success explicitly rather than converting every announced satellite into revenue.

### Five macro channels

Rates **1/5** raise financing and long-duration valuation costs; inflation **1/5** raises satellite, launch and equipment budgets. Liquidity **2/5** determines whether new capital is available before revenue scale. Cycle **3/5** supports connectivity demand but does not assure utilization; policy **1/5** captures spectrum, licensing, national security and launch approvals. Total **8/25** leaves a 50% normal-size cap and starter/add pacing outside conviction. **Evidence gap:** Future launch economics, approvals and financing conditions remain unknown; a macro score cannot repair an unfunded business forecast.

### Paying usage and network capacity required by the base

The $6.5 billion 2032 base is an analyst operating case, not disclosed contracted revenue. One explicit bridge is **130 million average paying user-equivalents at $4 per month net to AST**, plus **$260 million government/equipment revenue**. The mobile component is $6.24 billion annually. The $4 is after operator sharing: for example, $8 gross monthly spending with 50% retained by AST. Neither that retail price nor the sharing rate is verified contract economics. This adoption must be earned against alternative networks, and is not inferred from partner subscriber reach.

At an illustrative **0.25 GB monthly average traffic per paying user**,130 million users require **32.5 petabytes per month**. A hypothetical **200-satellite commercial fleet** would have to deliver around **0.50 Gbps average sellable throughput per satellite continuously**, before allowing for coverage holes and geographic demand mismatch. At50% effective utilization, engineered usable capacity must be about 1 Gbps per satellite on average. These are required capacity assumptions, not a claim that a 98.9 Mbps peak handset test proves aggregate network capacity. A 200-satellite fleet also requires replacement investment: at an illustrative $25 million fully deployed cost and seven-year service life, replacement alone would approach $714 million annually. The base's $1.2 billion 2032 capex leaves limited room for ground infrastructure and growth if those economics disappoint. Cumulative2027–32 base capex is $7.6 billion, which must fund expansion, launches and replacements.

**Evidence gap:** User ARPU, operator sharing, aggregate usable capacity, satellite cost and lifetime are not independently verified commercial results. The engineering/cash budget is therefore conditional rather than proof that130 million paying users can be served. Lower utilization or higher replacement cost should reduce value; the terminal cash-flow and capex sensitivities show that direction.
