## Investment thesis

AST SpaceMobile aims to connect ordinary mobile phones directly to satellites through partner mobile networks. A functioning constellation could earn wholesale revenue from coverage that terrestrial towers cannot serve economically. The investment depends on more than technical demonstrations: launch cadence, usable capacity, regulatory access, paying usage and funding must all work together. The October 28 assessment remains AVOID. Business quality is below the framework minimum on the available commercial and cash evidence, and the rebuilt weighted value is far below price. The strongest opposing case is that carrier distribution and a successful network rollout produce rapid adoption with high operating leverage. That is captured in the bull scenario, but it does not resolve present funding and execution uncertainty. A 40% adverse-gap stress reflects the binary character of this development path rather than a measured maximum loss.

## How the business creates value

Carrier partnerships could reduce customer-acquisition friction by placing satellite connectivity within existing mobile services. Revenue per user still depends on coverage quality, capacity, availability and commercial pricing after the carrier's share. Launches add usable network capacity only after deployment and performance validation, while satellites require manufacturing, spectrum coordination and eventual replacement. Competition from other satellite networks and terrestrial improvements may limit price. The FY2030 base assumes $3 billion revenue and 15% net margin; achieving it requires a commercial scale far beyond the latest reported revenue. Direct-to-phone performance, paying traffic and the cost per usable unit of capacity are the tests linking launches to value.

### Five macro channels

- **Rates 1/5:** Higher funding costs penalize both rollout finance and distant modeled profit.
- **Inflation 1/5:** Satellite, launch and labor costs can increase the cash needed per usable network unit.
- **Liquidity 4/5:** Access to equity or debt matters before the service becomes self-funding.
- **Cycle 2/5:** Coverage demand may persist through cycles, but carrier budgets and adoption still matter.
- **Policy 1/5:** Spectrum permissions, launch rules and cross-border service approvals directly affect access.

The resulting macro assessment is **9/25**, an uptrend under pressure. V5.2 applies a 50% cap on normal exposure and starter/add pacing in this band; it does not mechanically reduce intrinsic value or create a stock-selection veto. The October 28 Fed decision kept the policy range at 3.75–4.00%; the official ten-year Treasury par yield was 5.05%. High rates remain material even after the rebound in equity indices.

## What the price requires

Bear assumes a marginally profitable network with funding risk; base assumes a scaled carrier service; bull requires broad adoption and 25% net margin. Failure to fund the constellation could leave equity worth less than the displayed bear case, so it is not a liquidation floor.

Revenue is in USD billions. Dilution below is in millions of equity units. EPS = revenue in billions × 1,000 × net margin ÷ diluted units in millions; present value = EPS × exit P/E ÷ (1 + equity discount rate)^years.
