## Investment thesis

Coupang uses a dense logistics network and digital services to deliver goods quickly and encourage repeat customer spending. Greater order density can lower delivery cost per order, while newer services and overseas expansion can broaden revenue. The current investment case depends on a recovery in trust and operating profitability, not just higher sales. The latest quarter remained loss making even after excluding Korean administrative fines. The October 28 assessment is WATCH because the rebuilt weighted value is close to but below the quote and the credibility gate remains pending. The strongest opposing case is that the network's scale and recurring customers restore margins as exceptional costs fade. That requires evidence that underlying commerce profit can fund newer businesses and cash investment. Removing a fine from adjusted earnings does not by itself establish that recovery.

**Research date October 28, 2026.** Completed-session quote **$14.11**; independent comparison **$14.13**, difference 0.142%. After-hours prices are excluded from closing triggers. Model date October 28; terminal date 2028-12-31.

## How the business creates value

Fast delivery and a broad range can increase customer frequency and reduce fulfillment cost through local density. Product Commerce provides scale; restaurant delivery, international commerce and other offerings can add frequency but also consume cash before reaching scale. Competitive pricing limits how much cost improvement reaches net margin. Q2 Product Commerce active customers were 24.7 million, up 3%, while Developing Offerings still reported a substantial adjusted EBITDA loss. The FY2028 base assumes $48 billion sales and 2.5% net margin, materially above the current loss and requiring lower fulfillment cost, customer retention and better new-business economics. Pricing, logistics investment and privacy-related obligations must be assessed together.

### Five macro channels

- **Rates 1/5:** Higher rates affect consumer budgets and reduce the present value of a later margin recovery.
- **Inflation 1/5:** Fuel, wages and goods prices pressure delivery cost and household purchasing power.
- **Liquidity 4/5:** Financing access matters to expansion, though business cash generation remains the core test.
- **Cycle 2/5:** Consumer demand and discretionary spending affect order frequency and mix.
- **Policy 1/5:** Privacy enforcement, labor rules and cross-border regulation directly affect costs and trust.

The resulting macro assessment is **9/25**, an uptrend under pressure. V5.2 applies a 50% cap on normal exposure and starter/add pacing in this band; it does not mechanically reduce intrinsic value or create a stock-selection veto. The October 28 Fed decision kept the policy range at 3.75–4.00%; the official ten-year Treasury par yield was 5.05%. High rates remain material even after the rebound in equity indices.

**Evidence gap:** A complete product/customer concentration schedule, competitor order comparison and synchronized industry-wide estimate-revision history were not established. These omissions limit confidence in the forecast bridge and industry score; the next filing and peer results should test them.
