## Investment thesis

Coupang combines retail inventory, marketplace merchants, advertising and a dense delivery network, with newer investments in Taiwan, food delivery, luxury and other offerings. Its customer experience can earn increasing wallet share, but profitability depends on volume using the network, acquisition costs and disciplined expansion. The October 31 assessment is **AVOID at $14.37**, the October 30 close. A newly built fiscal 2028 model gives **$15.74 weighted present value**, only **9.5% upside**, while credibility remains **PENDING** after material privacy-control findings and unresolved remediation. The strongest opposing case is that customer spending returns, temporary marketing costs decline and Taiwan follows Korea's path to profitable scale. The model allows recovery, but does not convert management's description of temporary pressure into an assured cash outcome. [June 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1834584/000183458426000073/cpng-20260630.htm).

## Business economics and growth

Owned retail recognizes product revenue gross, while marketplace, advertising and other services have different revenue and margin economics. Delivery density, selection, customer trust and automation support retention; marketing and unused capacity can quickly reverse operating leverage. Product Commerce is principally Korea; Developing Offerings includes Taiwan and other businesses at different investment stages. Food delivery's progress does not establish that Taiwan is already profitable.

Second-quarter sales were **$8.856 billion versus $8.524 billion**, approximately 4% reported growth. Management reports **10% constant-currency growth**, reflecting a weaker won. Product Commerce revenue was **$7.425 billion versus $7.334 billion**, approximately 1% reported and 8% constant-currency growth; Developing Offerings **$1.431 billion versus $1.190 billion**, approximately 20% reported growth. Product Commerce gross profit fell from **$2.390 billion to $2.268 billion**; its adjusted EBITDA fell from **$663 million to $382 million**. Developing Offerings' adjusted loss improved to **$219 million from $235 million**, but remains a real economic cost.

The August call reports **24.7 million active Product Commerce customers** and WOW membership above the pre-incident level. Management says continuing and returning customers' spending is growing faster than the consolidated revenue line, and expects pre-incident Product Commerce adjusted EBITDA margins by **mid-2027**. These are management cohort interpretations and forecasts, not audited proof that all lost customers returned or that consolidation-wide margins have recovered. Q3 guidance was **8–9% constant-currency revenue growth**, **300–400 basis points adjusted EBITDA margin contraction year over year**, and full-year Developing Offerings adjusted losses **$950 million–$1 billion**. Holiday timing and seasonal costs can obscure recovery, but do not remove its execution risk. [Full August call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cpng/transcripts/660945-q2-2026/).

Naver's August 7 primary release reports service revenue growth of **31.3%** alongside expansion of Plus Store, membership and N Delivery. This establishes active competing commerce and logistics offerings; Naver's net services revenue is not directly comparable with Coupang's owned-inventory sales. Customer reacquisition must be earned against those alternatives. **Evidence gap:** Independently measured current wallet share, cohort retention and like-for-like merchant economics were not verified; no synchronized industry revision panel was established. [Naver primary results](https://navercorp.com/en/media/pressReleasesDetail?seq=10034587).
