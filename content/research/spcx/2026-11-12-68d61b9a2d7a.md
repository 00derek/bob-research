## Investment thesis

SpaceX combines broadband connectivity, external launch and compute services. Reuse and distribution can support its space economics; compute has a different competitive and replacement cycle, with cash commitments that can outlast a customer contract. The **November 12 assessment is TRIM**. The new model gives **$88.35 weighted present value (-36.8%)**, against the **$139.75 November 11 completed close**. The conditional security-level reduction target is 50% because price exceeds 115% of weighted value.

The November 10 Anthropic prospectus discloses that the $120.5 billion compute ceiling is cancellable after initial periods ending in January and March 2027, on 90 days’ notice. The new base case reduces compute revenue and margins and allows additional financing dilution; the ceiling is not guaranteed multiyear backlog. The strongest opposing case is that compute customers retain and expand their capacity despite cancellation rights, while efficient equipment use and connectivity profit fund the remaining investment. **Evidence gap:** Final Apollo closing, lease payment dates, collateral and residual-value terms, customer cancellation behavior and utilization cannot be established from the headline commitments alone. The extra equity raise is a stress assumption and may not be available.

## Business economics and competitive position

Connectivity customers pay for broadband coverage, mobility and government services. Reusable launch lowers the cost of deploying satellites and opens external missions, while compute customers rent powered infrastructure and software capabilities. These businesses reinforce engineering and distribution, but have different capital lives and margin risks. Internal Starlink launches are capitalized into satellites rather than counted again as external launch revenue. The SI segment includes cloud infrastructure, advertising/software and acquired Cursor; it is not all organic Grok subscriptions. Cash paid before delivery also creates future service obligations.

Rocket Lab's August Q2 release shows a smaller integrated rival generating $234 million quarterly revenue, with Neutron now targeting 2027. This supports SpaceX's scale advantage in launch, while government second-source procurement can sustain a competitor. Cloud infrastructure faces a different set of rivals and hardware depreciation: launch dominance cannot establish compute monopoly economics. [Rocket Lab Q2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819994/000181999426000061/rklb-08102026ex991.htm), [Neutron update](https://investors.rocketlabcorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/rocket-lab-provides-update-neutron-development-and-adjusts).

**Evidence gap:** Current product-level market share, contract margins and complete peer earnings revisions are not all disclosed or independently comparable. Competitive observations support the mechanism, but do not establish monopoly pricing or the exact terminal growth assumed.
