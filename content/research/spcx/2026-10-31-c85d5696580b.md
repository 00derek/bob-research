## Investment thesis

SpaceX combines launch services, Starlink connectivity and an expanding AI-compute/software business. Launch integration can lower the cost of building Starlink capacity; subscriptions can generate recurring cash, while AI offers a larger but capital-intensive growth path. The October 20 Q3 SEC release confirms Nasdaq SPCX represents Space Exploration Technologies common equity. Reported net profit is not enough to complete that bridge. The strongest opposing case is that recurring Starlink cash and contracted compute revenue finance expansion faster than the model captures. That requires cash after capex and funding claims, rather than treating adjusted EBITDA as distributable cash.

## How the business creates value

Space provides external launches and launches that build the company's own satellite network. Connectivity earns from consumer, enterprise and government Starlink service; SI sells compute capacity, models and software. Integration can create cost advantages, but internal launches are not third-party revenue and AI capacity requires large ongoing investment. One company multiple would obscure these different cash and capital characteristics.

The October 20 Q3 SEC release reports $14.575 billion revenue, $2.791 billion operating income and $4.4 billion net income. Connectivity generated $5.248 billion revenue and $2.367 billion operating income; SI generated $7.560 billion revenue but $431 million operating income. Total Q3 capex was $19.593 billion.
