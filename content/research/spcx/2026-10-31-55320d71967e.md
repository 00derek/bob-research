## Investment thesis

SpaceX now sells satellite connectivity, launch services and a fast-growing compute platform. The October 20 quarter shows strong demand and recurring Starlink profit, but enormous infrastructure spending means GAAP earnings and EBITDA do not describe cash left for shareholders. This new October 31 model resolves the current share and debt claims and explicitly funds the expansion. TRIM follows a price above 115% of the weighted estimate, requiring a conditional 50% valuation target despite substantial modeled growth. The strongest favorable case is that scarce powered compute and reusable launches let SpaceX earn superior returns for years. The opposing case is that rapid hardware replacement, rental-price competition and new debt absorb those returns. A potential Tesla combination is excluded from standalone values because there is no binding exchange ratio to model.

## How the business creates value

Connectivity customers pay for broadband coverage, mobility and government services. Reusable launch lowers the cost of deploying satellites and opens external missions, while compute customers rent powered infrastructure and software capabilities. These businesses reinforce engineering and distribution, but have different capital lives and margin risks. Internal Starlink launches are capitalized into satellites rather than counted again as external launch revenue. The SI segment includes cloud infrastructure, advertising/software and acquired Cursor; it is not all organic Grok subscriptions. Cash paid before delivery also creates future service obligations.

Rocket Lab's August Q2 release shows a smaller integrated rival generating $234 million quarterly revenue, with Neutron now targeting 2027. This supports SpaceX's scale advantage in launch, while government second-source procurement can sustain a competitor. Cloud infrastructure faces a different set of rivals and hardware depreciation: launch dominance cannot establish compute monopoly economics. [Rocket Lab Q2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819994/000181999426000061/rklb-08102026ex991.htm), [Neutron update](https://investors.rocketlabcorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/rocket-lab-provides-update-neutron-development-and-adjusts).

**Evidence gap:** Current product-level market share, contract margins and complete peer earnings revisions are not all disclosed or independently comparable. Competitive observations support the mechanism, but do not establish monopoly pricing or the exact terminal growth assumed.

## What could break the thesis

A large IPO cash balance does not remove financing risk when a single quarter can consume tens of billions. Lower compute rental prices or rapid obsolescence can compress margins before invested capital is recovered. Spectrum approvals, satellites, launches, AI power delivery and related-party contracts can fail together. Controlling ownership and a prospective combination also require minority-shareholder scrutiny. The model fails if utilization, retained cash and capital productivity do not improve with scale; more revenue alone is insufficient.

Business-quality gate **PASS at 22/30**; credibility gate **PASS**; no deep-value exception. Pre-profit flag **No**, based on current reported profitability rather than the historical label. Binary/event risk is **HIGH**; plausible gap stress **35%** is an analyst stress, not a maximum loss.

The observed sample of 98 adjusted sessions had a worst overnight gap of **-10.34% on 2026-08-05**, and worst daily return **-16.43% on 2026-06-22**. The chosen stress is larger than that sample's overnight decline, allowing for unobserved future events. Corporate actions and vendor history can distort samples, and even a long sample cannot bound a future loss.

**Evidence gap:** No exhaustive current legal/insider clearance or full event-loss distribution is established. Named control and funding concerns remain restrictions; the absence of an additional finding is not assurance of safety.
