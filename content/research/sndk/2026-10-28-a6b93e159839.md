## Investment thesis

Sandisk makes NAND flash and storage products used in data centers, edge devices and consumer electronics. Tight supply, higher selling prices and a shift toward enterprise products have produced extraordinary profits. For shareholders, the key issue is whether those margins persist after competitors add capacity or customers complete urgent purchases. HOLD at the October 28 close of $1,404.77 reflects modest upside to the retained FY2027 weighted estimate and a wide downside if pricing normalizes. No fresh entry qualifies ahead of tomorrow's result. The strongest opposing case is that new customer agreements and AI storage demand make the supply shortage more durable than past NAND cycles. The forecast already assumes unusually high margins, so that case must be tested against prices, bit shipments, committed contracts and cash after investment.

## How the business creates value

Sandisk designs memory and storage products and participates in manufacturing. Data-center SSD demand can improve mix, while capacity investment, technology transitions and yields affect bit cost and available supply. Selling price is central: higher shipments alone do not sustain the recent margin if NAND pricing reverses. Enterprise qualification can lengthen customer relationships, but NAND remains a capacity-sensitive market with strong competitors and technology substitution.

The August 5 FY2026 Q4 release reported $8.965 billion revenue versus $5.950 billion in Q3, 84.6% GAAP gross margin and $39.25 adjusted diluted EPS. FY2026 revenue was $20.248 billion and adjusted EPS $70.88. Management guided FY2027 Q1 revenue of $10.3–10.8 billion and adjusted EPS $44–46. The company authorized additional buybacks; authorization is not execution. The scenarios use adjusted earnings, whose exclusions must remain visible rather than being mislabeled GAAP net income. [Primary evidence](https://investor.sandisk.com/news-releases/news-release-details/sandisk-reports-fiscal-fourth-quarter-2026-financial-results).

The October 13 model's $45 billion base sales are near the annualized midpoint of the coming quarter's sales guide, with 65% adjusted net margin and 155 million diluted units. These are aggressive peak-profit assumptions, not a normalized through-cycle forecast. The 30% bear probability preserves meaningful downside to a $30 billion/35% margin case. This review repairs the reverse test: the bull forward case uses 12 x, so its inverse must use 12 x as well, rather than the earlier 10 x description. ## What could break the thesis

New capacity, customer inventory digestion or weaker AI capital spending could reduce NAND prices while factory costs remain. Rapid margin compression would reduce cash and also the multiple applied to earnings. An earnings beat does not invalidate the bear cycle case; watch realized pricing, customer commitments, capex discipline and inventory. A stronger contrary case would be binding multi-year contracts that preserve cash margins through additional supply.

Thesis **INTACT**; credibility **PASS**; GAAP pre-profit **No**; binary-event risk **HIGH**. The 25% plausible overnight gap is an analyst stress, not a forecast bound. A daily-close stop cannot guarantee execution at its level. For HIGH binary risk, the larger of stop distance and gap stress governs sizing.

**Evidence gap:** Complete current gap distributions, material capital claims and synchronized insider/short evidence are unavailable. Test the specific business failures above and refresh immediately on results, financing, governance or thesis-changing news.
