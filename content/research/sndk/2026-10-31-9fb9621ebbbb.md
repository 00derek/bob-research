## Investment thesis

October 29 results make the old forecast obsolete: fiscal Q1 revenue was $11.031 billion, GAAP gross margin 85.0% and adjusted EPS $49.51. Management guided next-quarter revenue to $12.2–12.6 billion and pre-split adjusted EPS $55–57. Those extraordinary margins support the demand thesis but require a new cycle-normalization and diluted-claim model before an entry assessment. PENDING_REFRESH replaces HOLD. The announced eight-for-one split is effective November 19 with adjusted trading November 20; all prices here remain pre-split. A split changes units, not economic value. [Q1 issuer release](https://investor.sandisk.com/news-releases/news-release-details/sandisk-reports-fiscal-first-quarter-2027-financial-results).

Sandisk makes NAND flash and storage products used in data centers, edge devices and consumer electronics. Tight supply, higher selling prices and a shift toward enterprise products have produced extraordinary profits. For shareholders, the key issue is whether those margins persist after competitors add capacity or customers complete urgent purchases. No fresh entry is established. The strongest opposing case is that new customer agreements and AI storage demand make the supply shortage more durable than past NAND cycles. The forecast already assumes unusually high margins, so that case must be tested against prices, bit shipments, committed contracts and cash after investment.

## How the business creates value

Sandisk designs memory and storage products and participates in manufacturing. Data-center SSD demand can improve mix, while capacity investment, technology transitions and yields affect bit cost and available supply. Selling price is central: higher shipments alone do not sustain the recent margin if NAND pricing reverses. Enterprise qualification can lengthen customer relationships, but NAND remains a capacity-sensitive market with strong competitors and technology substitution.

The August 5 FY2026 Q4 release reported $8.965 billion revenue versus $5.950 billion in Q3, 84.6% GAAP gross margin and $39.25 adjusted diluted EPS. FY2026 revenue was $20.248 billion and adjusted EPS $70.88. Management guided FY2027 Q1 revenue of $10.3–10.8 billion and adjusted EPS $44–46. The company authorized additional buybacks; authorization is not execution. The scenarios use adjusted earnings, whose exclusions must remain visible rather than being mislabeled GAAP net income. ## What could break the thesis

New capacity, customer inventory digestion or weaker AI capital spending could reduce NAND prices while factory costs remain. Rapid margin compression would reduce cash and also the multiple applied to earnings. An earnings beat does not invalidate the bear cycle case; watch realized pricing, customer commitments, capex discipline and inventory. A stronger contrary case would be binding multi-year contracts that preserve cash margins through additional supply.
