## Investment thesis

Sandisk sells NAND storage used to keep data accessible when AI workloads exceed expensive fast-memory capacity. The new quarter shows enterprise demand converting into unusually high profit and cash, yet customers and competitors can eventually reverse the shortage. The new October 31 model normalizes FY2029 net margin to 50% in the base case, well below current reported profitability, and still gives about 34% upside at the October 30 close. WATCH is the current security assessment. A future completed close within the newly declared $1120–1170 pre-split zone, with $1020 structural downside, could qualify a BUY_STARTER if all other gates still pass; the October30 close cannot retroactively satisfy a zone first declared October31. This is a valuation-led starter with very weak timing, not permission to scale. The strongest opposing case is a much faster collapse in NAND prices: the bear estimate is far below market. The favorable alternative is that durable customer commitments sustain pricing and production returns for longer than modeled.

## How the business creates value

Datacenter SSDs combine NAND capacity, controllers and firmware into qualified systems. Training and inference retain checkpoints, retrieval data and increasingly context; this increases bytes stored without guaranteeing revenue per bit. Sandisk shares wafer manufacturing with Kioxia, so bit supply, process transitions and partner funding matter as much as end demand. BiCS upgrades can lower cost per bit while enterprise qualification supports differentiation. Samsung, SK hynix and Micron remain capable suppliers; higher industry capacity can eventually transfer value from producers to buyers. The new model does not assign a separate speculative premium to high-bandwidth flash.

Micron reported fiscal Q4 2026 core data-center revenue of $18.002 billion versus $11.524 billion sequentially, with 90% gross margin. That segment includes products beyond NAND, so it does not establish Sandisk market share; it does corroborate strong supplier economics across the memory cycle. Its investment in more capacity is also a reason not to capitalize peak NAND profits indefinitely. [Micron September 30 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000018/a2026q4ex991-pressrelease.htm). **Evidence gap:** A synchronized NAND-only supply, cost and market-share panel was not reconstructed. Product-level customer pricing and competitive qualification remain undisclosed; those gaps limit industry confidence despite reported demand.

## What the price requires

Net margin 50% assumes meaningful normalization from current adjusted profitability. Bear $40 bn/35% stresses lower pricing and utilization even as bit demand remains; bull $85 bn/60% needs persistent supply discipline and enterprise mix. The 8/10/12 x terminal earnings range recognizes peak-cycle risk. No investment gains are included.

The base revenue schedule is FY2027 $50 billion, FY2028 $57 billion and FY2029 $65 billion: approximately 14% annual growth after the near-term $49.6 billion run rate. It requires additional enterprise bits and mix while allowing lower pricing; the source does not promise this trajectory. For normalized earnings, assume 68% gross margin rather than current 85%, operating expense of 8% of sales and an effective tax rate of 16.7% with negligible net interest: (68% − 8%) × (1 − 16.7%) ≈ 50% net margin. Bear uses 52% gross margin, 10% expense and 16.7% tax to reach about 35%; bull uses 78%, 6% and 16.7% to reach about 60%. These are explicitly analyst assumptions, not proof that scarcity persists; five points of net margin changes base value by $156 per share. The unusually low current terminal multiples are the other major normalization control.

## What could break the thesis

The main downside is correlated price and volume disappointment: customers finish urgent purchases just as new supply reaches the market. Inventory and receivables then absorb cash while venture commitments persist. A fall in recurring margin, adverse contract renegotiation, rising customer delinquencies or capacity expansion without committed returns would undermine the base. The bull case needs profitable demand, not merely more bits. A 25% overnight gap is a stress assumption; actual loss can exceed it.

Business-quality **PASS at 26/30**; credibility **PASS within reviewed disclosures**; no deep-value exception. Pre-profit **No**. Binary-event **HIGH** and assumed gap stress **25%**. These are separate gates and judgments. Reported controls/transaction disclosure is not the same as exhaustive clearance of every legal or insider issue.

**Evidence gap:** Full current institutional 13F changes and complete event-gap distributions were attempted through issuer/statistics/insider sources but not reconstructed. No flow confirmation or guaranteed maximum loss is claimed.
