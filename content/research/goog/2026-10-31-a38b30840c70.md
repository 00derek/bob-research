## Investment thesis

Alphabet converts search intent into advertising, sells enterprise computing and develops distribution through Android, Chrome, YouTube and its AI products. Those advantages are real, but the equity must fund an exceptionally expensive infrastructure expansion. The newly rebuilt October 31 assessment is **EXIT at the October 30 close of $278.22**. A cash-flow model that actually includes the spending gives **$155.60 weighted present value**. The close also breaches the previously published $280 structural closing reference. This conclusion rests on cash conversion and price structure; it does not require asserting that Google has lost search or that AI demand is imaginary. The strongest opposing case is that current investment buys highly profitable, durable capacity and spending subsequently normalizes. The bull explicitly models that outcome and reaches **$317.38**. [Full September 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm).

## Business economics and the latest operating evidence

Advertisers buy access to users with commercial intent; enterprise customers buy compute, storage, software and AI capacity. Data, distribution, developer integration and switching costs support the franchise, while alternative search interfaces and cloud suppliers contest its economics. AI can improve product usefulness and add cloud demand, but answering queries also consumes compute. Revenue growth therefore benefits shareholders only if pricing and utilization ultimately exceed infrastructure, energy, model-development and distribution costs.

September revenue was **$127.577 billion**, versus **$102.346 billion** a year earlier, and operating income **$40.576 billion**, versus **$31.228 billion**. Services generated **$96.302 billion revenue and $39.157 billion operating income**. Cloud generated **$30.709 billion revenue and $11.121 billion operating income**, approximately **36.2% margin**. Search revenue of **$63.350 billion** grew approximately 12%; that is slower than the prior quarter's 17% growth. Cloud's 103% growth includes substantial TPU hardware revenue, so treating the quarter as pure recurring software revenue would overstate comparability. Other Bets lost **$2.047 billion**, while shared Alphabet AI costs lost **$7.655 billion**. [Quarterly release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000080/googexhibit991q32026.htm); [segment tables](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm).

Management's call reports **$561 billion cloud backlog**, with just over 55% expected to convert during the next 24 months. That supports demand but does not make the entire backlog next-year sales. Third-party capacity became available only late in the quarter, with limited current effect and a greater fourth-quarter margin headwind. Management reiterated **$195–205 billion 2026 capex** and said 2027 would be significantly higher without supplying a precise number. Depreciation, energy and data-center operations subsequently enter expenses as well. [October 28 call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/googl/transcripts/712854-q3-2026/).

AWS, Azure, AI model providers and alternative advertising channels can pressure pricing and distribution. Alphabet's integrated TPU, software and customer distribution are advantages; they do not establish a perpetual return on every new data center. **Evidence gap:** An independently synchronized cloud-capacity, utilization and peer-return-on-investment panel was not verified. Current backlog is strong issuer evidence, while future industry pricing and durable hardware mix remain assumptions. This distinction is central to the scenario margins and capex paths.

Microsoft’s October 28 primary release reports **$33.165 billion Azure revenue,48% growth**, and MicrosoftCloud growth of 30%. That corroborates cloud demand while showing vigorous rival capacity and product competition. Microsoft changed its segment presentation inSeptember, so old and new segment margins are not compared mechanically. These results do not prove that Google lost share: product mix, accounting and starting scale differ. [Microsoft primary results](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2027-q1/press-release-webcast).

## Financial health: earnings are not the cash flow

Net income attributable to common was **$26.170 billion**, below **$34.979 billion**, despite higher operating income. Investment income moved from a **$10.734 billion gain to an $8.664 billion loss**. Forecasting GAAP EPS without separating those marks obscures operating performance. Quarter interest income of **$1.768 billion** and interest expense of **$0.967 billion** are financing items. Another **$0.405 billion capitalized interest** is deferred into assets rather than disappearing economically. The valuation therefore uses operating profit and an explicit financing bridge.

Quarterly operating cash of **$42.290 billion** was less than **$51.360 billion capex**, giving **negative $9.070 billion CFO less capex**. Nine-month operating cash was **$127.149 billion**, versus **$112.311 billion**, but capex rose from **$63.596 billion to $131.953 billion**. Nine-month free cash flow consequently fell from **positive $48.715 billion to negative $4.804 billion**. Stock compensation was **$23.338 billion**, and year-to-date repurchases were **zero**. Current growth is being funded rather than automatically returned to each share. [Cash-flow and capital tables](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm).

Cash and marketable securities were **$265.098 billion**, with **$125.102 billion debt**, **$3.705 billion finance leases** and **$13.807 billion accrued regulatory fines**. Nonmarketable investments of **$135.230 billion** are separate from operating value and receive substantial scenario haircuts. Operating leases already reduce modeled EBIT, so subtracting their entire reported balance again would mix valuation conventions. The **$130.5 billion future lease-payment commitment for not-yet-commenced leases** creates substantial expansion obligations, but is neither a present-value number nor automatically additional current debt. The forecast's reinvestment assumptions must carry the associated economic cost.

### Preferred conversion and common dilution

Alphabet issued **$19.25 billion face amount** of mandatory convertible preferred securities, represented by depositary shares. Their approximately **$18.023 billion accounting carrying amount** follows fees and capped-call costs; it is not the liquidation face claim. The **6.25% dividend** requires approximately **$1.203 billion annually** until conversion in May 2029. The stated Class A and C conversion endpoints imply roughly **54.46 million maximum common shares**. The cash-flow model deducts dividends through conversion, includes the maximum conversion shares in the diluted denominator and does not also subtract preferred principal after conversion. Capped-call benefits are excluded conservatively rather than guaranteed.

September common shares were approximately **12.294 billion**, alongside **284 million unvested RSUs/PSUs** and **$61.8 billion unrecognized compensation expense** over an estimated 2.8 years. The quarter issued approximately **36 million common shares for $12.4 billion net** under an at-the-market program, with **$27.6 billion capacity remaining**. The fixed terminal denominator is **12.63246 billion** in all three cases: 12.294 billion existing common plus 284 million outstanding awards plus 54.46 million maximum preferred conversion shares. No future ATM issuance or additional award dilution is assumed, and no unmodeled financing proceeds are added. Forecast operating margins treat future compensation as a cash-equivalent expense for future services rather than adding it back; existing awards are included in the starting diluted claim. This is an explicit compensation convention, not a projection of unchanged GAAP shares or a second forecast dilution penalty. [Equity, debt, compensation and preferred notes](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm).

**Evidence gap:** Future settlement prices, capped-call benefits, new stock awards, ATM issuance and the legal outcomes underlying fines are unknown. The model uses claim-consistent endpoints rather than one current share count for all future years. The liquidity buffer supports a quality-gate PASS; it does not make future reinvestment free.

## Rebuilt cash-flow valuation

It starts from September statements, models remaining 2026 cash use, and forecasts 2027–2030 sales, operating margins, depreciation, capex and working capital. Cash flow is operating profit × **82% after tax**, plus depreciation, minus capex and working-capital investment. No stock-compensation addback is taken.

The model deliberately remains capital intensive: terminal capex exceeds depreciation by **11%/9%/7% of sales** in bear/base/bull. The enterprise discount rates are assumed capital costs, not quoted equity returns. Each cash flow is discounted using its actual year-end distance from October 31; the terminal date is **4.1670 years** away. The equity bridge adds cash and haircut investments, deducts debt, finance leases and accrued fines, then deducts preferred cash dividends through May 2029. No preferred principal is deducted again after maximum-share conversion. The September cash bridge requires a timing caveat: full fourth-quarter cash consumption is included even though October has elapsed and its actual cash movement is unavailable. Reducing the stub to two-thirds, approximately November–December, raises weighted value from **$155.60 to $156.30**; omitting it entirely gives **$157.68**. The timing uncertainty does not drive the conclusion.

### Reverse valuation and sensitivities

At the same bull discount, terminal growth, capital bridge, dilution and interim cash flows, $278.22 requires approximately **$230.70 billion 2031 free cash flow**, below the identical bull's **$269.66 billion**. Reverse gate **PASS** means the optimistic case can support the price. It is compatible with the forward model, but does not mean weighted expected return is positive.

Reducing base capex by five percentage points of annual sales, including terminal reinvestment, raises base value to **$196.31**; increasing it five points lowers value to **$107.21**. Base discount rates of **9% and 11%** give approximately **$178.87 and $131.52**. These wide differences explain the importance of utilization, asset life and actual cash spending. They also show that a superficial EPS beat is insufficient to justify the quote.

**Evidence gap:** Depreciation lives, future power/lease costs, hardware mix, investment realizations and terminal reinvestment remain uncertain. The explicit paths permit review; they are not a precision claim. A material fall in cash investment at sustained growth would warrant rebuilding the valuation upward.
