## Investment thesis

Broadcom sells custom compute, networking chips and infrastructure software into customers whose switching and qualification costs can support attractive returns. The deep review now supplies an explicit valuation instead of leaving the new financing backstop unmodeled. At **$326.08 on October 30**, the new October 31 scenarios give **$432.07 weighted present value**, approximately **32.5% upside**. The current signal remains **WATCH**: the newly defined accumulation zone requires a subsequent daily close, and the latest close cannot satisfy a rule that was not yet declared. The strongest opposing case is that AI customers' financing needs indicate demand beyond their ability to pay, while memory content and rack commitments dilute Broadcom's economics. Those risks enter both operating scenarios and a separate contingent-loss calculation. [Latest full 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000173016826000080/avgo-20260802.htm).

## Business economics and demand

Custom accelerators can improve performance and power consumption for a customer's specific workloads; networking connects those systems, while enterprise software generates recurring revenue. Design integration and qualification create switching costs, but a small set of hyperscalers and AI laboratories controls large purchasing budgets. Growth depends on completed designs, foundry/package capacity, memory, power and customer finance—not just announced compute demand.

September 2 results cover the quarter ending **August 2**, not September. Revenue rose from **$15.952 billion to $29.591 billion**, operating income from **$5.887 billion to $15.955 billion**, and GAAP net income from **$4.140 billion to $13.088 billion**. AI semiconductor revenue reached **$16.7 billion**, versus a **$21.7 billion fourth-quarter outlook**; total fourth-quarter revenue guidance is **$34.8 billion**. Management's $115 billion fiscal 2027 and $230 billion fiscal 2028 AI trajectory is unusually large and supply-backed according to management, but is not a guarantee. [Issuer results](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-inc-announces-third-quarter-fiscal-year-2026-financial); [September call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/09/09/broadcom-avgo-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/).

Mix matters. The call's non-GAAP gross margin falls from about **75% to 73%** in the fourth-quarter outlook as memory-intensive accelerators expand, while non-GAAP operating margin is guided to **66%**. The new model therefore does not simply apply the reported quarter's net margin to the entire 2028 revenue trajectory. Nvidia's integrated platforms and alternative custom-chip suppliers contest these budgets; customers can also redesign internally. Software licensing changes invite customer migration and regulatory challenge. **Evidence gap:** Independent supplier-share, customer-return and synchronized peer-revision tables were not verified. Management's demand visibility and competitor/platform announcements are useful evidence, not proof of a permanent monopoly.

Total **8/25** keeps the 50% normal-size cap and starter/add pacing, separate from conviction. **Evidence gap:** Precise sensitivities and policy outcomes are not known; customer finance is a specific transmission mechanism rather than a generic macro haircut.

## Financial health and the actual backstop

Nine-month operating cash was **$32.950 billion**, versus **$19.834 billion**, and cash capex **$1.013 billion**, versus **$0.386 billion**. CFO less capex was therefore **$31.937 billion**, versus **$19.448 billion**. Stock compensation of **$6.287 billion** contributed to operating cash reconciliation; it is not a free recurring benefit in the earnings valuation. Buybacks consumed **$8.450 billion** and dividends **$9.281 billion**. Cash was **$23.975 billion**, against **$59.419 billion debt**, before the subsequent debt repayments discussed on the call. Receivables of **$13.707 billion** and inventory **$4.523 billion** rose sharply, tying cash to growth. Quarterly diluted shares were **4.887 billion**; common shares were **4.774 billion** and outstanding RSUs **165 million**. No preferred shares were outstanding. [Cash-flow, balance-sheet and equity notes](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000173016826000080/avgo-20260802.htm).

The October 27 8-K resolves important terms of the residual-value support. Potential loss equals **72.5% of outstanding lease amounts minus the proceeds realized from AI racks**, subject to the contract's remedies. Exposure rises with deliveries and declines with customer lease payments. Remedies include assuming the lease, conditional resale to the seller at a fixed price, or arranging an asset sale. The **$43 billion maximum undiscounted liability** is neither current cash debt nor an amount that can be assumed worthless. Management regards triggering as unlikely; that view is not a measured default probability. [Actual 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000119312526434271/d148323d8k.htm).

To make the uncertainty reviewable, the model assumes full deployment's maximum implies **$59.31 billion outstanding leases** ($43 billion ÷ 72.5%). Bear/base/bull default probabilities are **40%/15%/5%**, and rack recoveries are **30%/50%/70% of outstanding leases**. Applying max(72.5% × leases − recovery, 0), then multiplying by default probability, gives **$10.083/$2.002/$0.074 billion expected contingent losses**. These losses are deducted once at the 2028 endpoint; ordinary margins exclude this separate credit loss. Commercial weakness and credit loss can occur together in bear. The probabilities and recoveries are deliberately visible analyst judgments, not contractually promised protections.

**Evidence gap:** The complete executed underlying agreements, customer credit model, timing of deployment and realizable secondhand hardware prices are not fully public. The reserve replaces an unexplained zero-loss assumption, but does not make the claim certain. A full $43 billion loss would remove roughly $6.99 per present share at this endpoint, illustrating both the direct claim's scale and why lost operating revenue could be more important.

### Forward cash feasibility

The base's **$290 billion fiscal 2028 revenue and 37% common net margin** produce **$107.3 billion earnings** after financing, ordinary tax and cash-equivalent compensation. A feasibility budget adds **$8 billion depreciation/amortization**, deducts **$10 billion working capital** and **$3 billion capex**, leaving approximately **$102.3 billion cash after investment**. Dividends of $12 billion and $15 billion debt repayment leave $75.3 billion before backstop settlements or other commitments. Earmarking another $2 billion for the separately modeled base expected backstop loss leaves $73.3 billion. This earmark is a cash-use test, not a second valuation subtraction. A fiscal 2027 bridge using $210 billion sales/35% net margin, $7 billion depreciation, $8 billion working capital and $2.5 billion capex yields $70 billion cash after investment; $11 billion dividends, $10 billion debt repayment and $2 billion backstop liquidity leave $47 billion.

This budget is conditional on the revenue and margin ramp. It does not assume unspecified acquisitions or extra buybacks to justify the **4.95 billion fixed claim denominator**. Future compensation remains a cash-equivalent cost, so no extra future award dilution or compensation addback is layered on top. Even a $20 billion additional working-capital draw plus the full $43 billion backstop amount is within one modeled terminal year's pre-distribution cash, but would consume most of that headroom; timing could be much worse than annual totals. **Evidence gap:** Customer delivery terms, chip purchasing commitments, acquired-intangible amortization and actual settlement timing may differ materially; the budget demonstrates conditional funding capacity, not an assured cash forecast.
