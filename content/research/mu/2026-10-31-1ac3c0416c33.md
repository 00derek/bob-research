## Investment thesis

Micron converts manufacturing capacity and technology into memory used in servers, consumer devices and industrial systems. AI systems use both more memory and higher-value high-bandwidth memory, so constrained supply can increase revenue and margins together. The September 30 results show that this demand is already producing cash, rather than merely an order-book narrative. The investment still requires discipline: memory pricing is cyclical, and exceptional margins can attract new capacity or encourage substitution. At $884.74 on October 30, the $1,277.27 weighted scenario estimate offers about 44% upside, but reward/risk to the $755 planning stop is 3.03:1 and the price is above the $850–880 starter zone but below the $900 outer full ceiling. The assessment is **Watch**. The strongest opposing case is a supply response or customer digestion that compresses prices before the earnings multiple expands. The old entry cannot be chased after the rebound.

## How the business creates value

HBM packaging, advanced DRAM and NAND require manufacturing expertise, yield control and large capital commitments. More bytes and bandwidth per AI system can support pricing, while allocation of capacity to HBM may constrain other memory. These are economic links, not proof that all products can retain peak margins. Rival supply, China competition and customer inventory remain the principal cycle counterweights.

The [September 30 FY2026 Q4 release](https://investors.micron.com/news/press-release/2026/Micron-Technology-Inc--Reports-Record-Fiscal-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx) reports $54.23 billion quarterly revenue, up from $41.46 billion the prior quarter, and $32.87 GAAP diluted EPS. Quarterly operating cash was $43.97 billion and net capex $10.77 billion; issuer-adjusted free cash flow was $33.20 billion. Full-year revenue was $133.19 billion. Management's FY2027 Q1 revenue guide is $61.5 billion±$1.5 billion, with adjusted EPS$38.15±$1.00. These are exceptional outcomes, not a perpetual earnings floor.

The base FY2027 $230 billion sales assumption is below four times the new quarterly guide, while55% net margin normalizes below the latest quarter's roughly 70% GAAP net margin. Cash/investments/restricted cash $73.48 billion provide financial strength, but restricted funds, customer advances and future capex are not added wholesale to equity value. **Evidence gap:** A full advance-payment, capacity, maintenance/growth capex and customer-concentration schedule is incomplete; verify how much current profitability and cash conversion can survive a weaker pricing cycle.

## Macro transmission

Rates affect the discount on future memory earnings and customers' ability to finance compute infrastructure. Energy and equipment inflation can raise fab costs, while capital liquidity can fund both customer demand and rival supply. Memory remains cyclical alongside secular AI content growth. Export restrictions, regional capacity subsidies and China competition can change supply and addressable markets.

The October 31 shared macro assessment is **8/25**: rates 1/5, inflation 1/5, liquidity 2/5, cycle 3/5 and policy 1/5. September PCE released October 29 showed 3.6% headline and 3.1% core annual inflation, while third-quarter real GDP grew at a 3.4% annual rate. Persistent inflation and growth leave long-duration earnings exposed to discount-rate pressure. The 50% normal-size cap and starter/add-only limit remain; macro modifies size rather than company conviction. ## What could break the thesis

The strongest opposing case is that current earnings are closer to a cyclical peak than a durable base. New industry capacity, customers reducing inventories or cheaper alternative memory could cut both volume and pricing; a lower multiple can compound the loss. Watch HBM qualification and shipments, pricing outside HBM, capacity commitments and cash after capital spending. A persistent revenue/margin miss below the bear path or loss of key technology qualifications would require a thesis review. The 20% adverse-gap assumption is not a bound on losses.
